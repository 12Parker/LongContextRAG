\subsection{A correct neutral flag hurts on misinformation and helps on genuine conflicts}
\label{sec:neutral}
Flagging exactly the gold contradictions, in the neutral format, \emph{lowers} gpt-4o-mini's accuracy on conflict
questions by \adOracleFlagConf\ points \dOracleFlagConfCI, from \plainConf\%\ (H2). With gpt-4.1 the drop is
\agptOracleFlagConf\ points (H9). Both hold under either metric. The errors are not refusals. With the flag, the share of
conflict answers that include the misinformation answer rises from \wrongPlain\% to \wrongOracleFlag\%, while gold
recall barely moves (\goldPlain\% to \goldOracleFlag\%). The model lists the misinformation \emph{alongside} the
correct answer, treating ``these passages conflict'' as ``both are valid''. That reading is right when both sides are valid. On WikiContradict, where the
reference answer is both sides, the same oracle flag \emph{raises} the share of answers that give both sides by
\awikiOracleFlag\ points \wikiOracleFlagCI\ with gpt-4o-mini, and by \awikiGptOracleFlag\ points \wikiGptOracleFlagCI\
with gpt-4.1 (H8; Table~\ref{tab:wiki}). A neutral flag passes on \emph{that} a conflict exists, but not \emph{which
kind} it is. Whether it helps depends on whether listing both sides is the right behaviour.

\subsection{Direction is what helps}
Telling the model \emph{which} side is likely wrong reverses the effect (H3): the share of answers that include the
misinformation falls to \wrongOracleDir\% with the directional flag and \wrongOracleRem\% with removal. With oracle flags, the directional
format gains \adOracleDirConf\ points on conflict questions \dOracleDirConfCI, and removing the suspect passage gains
\adOracleRemConf\ \dOracleRemConfCI. Both are robust to the metric and replicate with gpt-4.1 (\gptOracleDirConf\ and
\gptOracleRemConf; H9, Table~\ref{tab:gpt41}).

\subsection{What a detector must get right}
\label{sec:grid}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{generated/fig_grid.pdf}
\caption{Synthetic detectors at recall 1: change in one-way strict accuracy (points) against no flags, by pair
precision (rows) and direction accuracy (columns), for the directional flag (top) and removal (bottom). $^*$:
significant after Holm correction over the 36 cells of that mode and subset. Clean questions only see false flags,
so their rows do not vary with direction accuracy.}
\label{fig:grid}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:grid} replaces the oracle with synthetic detectors of known quality. Two thresholds appear. First,
\emph{direction accuracy}: at a direction accuracy of 0.5 (a coin flip on which side is wrong), no cell gains
significantly in either format. Only perfect direction gives a significant gain on all questions (directional flag
\gDirOneDOneAll, removal \gRemOneDOneAll\ points at perfect precision). Second, \emph{precision}, but only for removal:
false flags make the model delete correct passages, which costs clean questions \agRemHalfDOneClean\ points at
precision 0.5 and \agRemQuDOneClean\ at 0.25. False directional flags cost clean questions at most two points. Across
all grid cells, the harm on clean questions tracks the share of clean questions that lose a correct document
(Spearman $\rho=\hFourRho$, $p<0.001$), and \hFourHarm\ of \hFourCells\ false-flag cells are significantly harmful (H4).
Removal is the more powerful and the more fragile interface: its best cell gains twice the directional flag's on all
questions, but below precision 0.75 it is the only format that does net harm.

\subsection{Real detectors}
\label{sec:real}
\input{generated/tab_detectors}

Table~\ref{tab:detectors} describes the detectors. The pre-registered F1 ordering (H1) holds only in part. NLI beats
CAAG (\fOneNli\ vs.\ \fOneCaag), but the LLM detector's F1 (\fOneLlm) is not significantly higher than NLI's. Its
direction accuracy is \dirAccLlm\%, far above chance. NLI and the LLM detector flag most clean questions (\cleanFprNli\% and \cleanFprLlm\%); CAAG flags \cleanFprCaag\%.

The detectors' downstream effects differ far more than their F1 scores. In the neutral format, no real detector helps:
CAAG, NLI and the LLM detector all stay within a few points of no flags. CAAG performs no differently from random flags
at its own flag rate (H5; \dCaagFlagAll\ vs.\ \dRandFlagAll\ points overall), so its pair selections carry no usable
signal. Directional output changes the picture. The LLM detector gains \adLlmDirConf\ points on conflict questions with
directional flags, and \adLlmRemConf\ with removal. Removal, however, costs \adLlmRemClean\ on clean questions, so its
overall effect is \dLlmRemAll. The directional flag costs \adLlmDirClean\ on clean questions and comes out
\dLlmDirAll\ points ahead overall. With gpt-4.1 as the generator, the same gpt-4o-mini detector gives a significant
overall gain with directional flags (\gptLlmDirAll\ points, no loss on clean questions), while removal again nets out
near zero (\gptLlmRemAll). For reference, MADAM-RAG gains \adMadamAll\ points overall at roughly nine times the prompt
tokens. CAAG in its original typed and scored format behaves the same as in the uniform format (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}).

\subsection{Document-level behaviour predicts a detector's effect; pair-level F1 does not}
\label{sec:placement-results}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{generated/fig_placement.pdf}
\caption{Effect of real detectors predicted from the synthetic grid against the measured effect (with 95\% CIs), for
conflict ($\bullet$) and clean ($\blacksquare$) questions. Left: detectors located by their document-level behaviour;
right: by pair precision, recall and direction accuracy.}
\label{fig:placement}
\end{figure}

If the grid only described synthetic detectors, it would be of limited use. Figure~\ref{fig:placement} tests whether
it predicts real ones (H6). Locating each real detector by its pair-level precision, recall and direction accuracy
puts \hSixPairIn\ of \hSixN\ effects inside their 95\% CIs (mean absolute error \hSixPairMae\ points). The LLM
detector is the clearest failure: its pair recall is low, yet it removes the misinformation on \misGoneLlm\% of
conflict questions, because one correctly directed pair is enough to remove a misinformation document. Locating
detectors by what they do to documents (the share of conflict questions whose misinformation is removed, and the share
of clean questions that lose a correct document) puts \hSixDocIn\ of \hSixN\ inside (error \hSixDocMae\ points). We
chose these axes after seeing the LLM detector on the pilot split. The confirmatory test covers three detectors and
two formats.

\subsection{Prompt-only baselines depend on wording}
Neither instruction wording changes RAMDocs accuracy significantly after correction (H7). Their behaviour on
WikiContradict explains why prompting alone is not a fix. The v1 instruction, which allows ``genuinely different valid
answers'', raises both-sides answers there by \awikiInstrOne\ points; v2, which asks the model to drop unreliable
passages, lowers them by \awikiInstrTwo. Each wording builds in one policy for handling conflicts, and that policy is
right for one kind of conflict and wrong for the other. A directional detector can make that choice per pair.

\paragraph{CONFLICTS.} On the 237 automatically scorable CONFLICTS questions (exploratory, one seed), no condition
moves accuracy significantly; all are within $\pm$5 points (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}). Most of these items have no
conflict (168), and 62 of the 69 conflict items involve outdated information.
