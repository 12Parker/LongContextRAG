\subsection{A correct neutral flag does not help against misinformation}
\label{sec:neutral}
\input{generated/tab_generality}
Flagging exactly the gold contradictions, in the neutral format, \emph{lowers} gpt-4o-mini's accuracy on conflict
questions by \adOracleFlagConf\ points \dOracleFlagConfCI, from \plainConf\%\ (H2). With gpt-4.1 the drop is
\agptOracleFlagConf\ points (H9). Both hold under either metric. The errors are not refusals. With the flag, the share
of conflict answers that include the misinformation answer rises from \wrongPlain\% to \wrongOracleFlag\%, while gold
recall barely moves (\goldPlain\% to \goldOracleFlag\%): the model lists the misinformation \emph{alongside} the
correct answer.

\paragraph{Not an artefact of the prompt.} Our default skeleton asks for every valid answer, which could invite
exactly this behaviour. Under the minimal skeleton, which drops that policy, the oracle neutral flag still lowers
accuracy on conflict questions in all three generators (Table~\ref{tab:generality}): by \axMiniMinOracleFlagConf\
points with gpt-4o-mini and \axFourMinOracleFlagConf\ with gpt-4.1 (both significant after Holm; H10), and by
\axQwenMinOracleFlagConf\ with Qwen2.5-7B (not significant). With the default skeleton Qwen's drop is also
\axQwenOracleFlagConf\ points (not significant; H11). The estimate is negative in all six generator--skeleton
settings. What changes is the mechanism. Without the answer policy, gpt-4o-mini no longer adds the misinformation
(\wrongMiniMinPlain\% to \wrongMiniMinOracleFlag\%) but drops the correct answer (gold recall \goldMiniMinPlain\% to
\goldMiniMinOracleFlag\%). A neutral flag says that something is wrong without saying what, and the model either
keeps both sides or loses the right one.

\paragraph{The same flag helps on genuine conflicts.} On WikiContradict, where the reference answer is both sides,
the oracle flag \emph{raises} the share of answers that give both sides by \awikiOracleFlag\ points
\wikiOracleFlagCI\ with gpt-4o-mini, and by \awikiGptOracleFlag\ points \wikiGptOracleFlagCI\ with gpt-4.1 (H8;
Table~\ref{tab:wiki}). A neutral flag passes on \emph{that} a conflict exists but not \emph{which kind} it is, and
whether it helps depends on whether keeping both sides is the right behaviour.

\subsection{Acting on direction helps; a directional flag helps when the model follows it}
\label{sec:direction}
Given the same oracle pairs, naming the misinformation side beats the neutral flag on conflict questions in all six
generator--skeleton settings (H3, H9--H11; Table~\ref{tab:hypotheses}). Against no flags, \emph{removing} the suspect
passage gains in every setting, from \axQwenMinOracleRemConf\ to \agptOracleRemConf\ points, all significant after
Holm. The directional \emph{flag} is less consistent. It gains \adOracleDirConf\ points with gpt-4o-mini
\dOracleDirConfCI\ and \agptOracleDirConf\ with gpt-4.1 under the default skeleton, and \axFourMinOracleDirConf\ with
gpt-4.1 under the minimal one. But with gpt-4o-mini under the minimal skeleton (\xMiniMinOracleDirConf) and with
Qwen2.5-7B (\xQwenOracleDirConf, \xQwenMinOracleDirConf) the gain is not significant. Qwen largely ignores the
header: the share of its answers that include the misinformation falls only from \wrongQwenPlain\% to
\wrongQwenOracleDir\%, against \wrongPlain\% to \wrongOracleDir\% for gpt-4o-mini.

\paragraph{Where the directional flag fails (exploratory).} After seeing the minimal-skeleton run, we split conflict
questions by the number of gold answers. \nConfMulti\ of \the\numexpr\nConfMulti+\nConfSingle\relax\ have several
(same-name ambiguity \emph{and} misinformation), and a strict answer must list all of them. On the \nConfSingle\
single-answer conflict questions, the directional flag's estimate is positive in every setting, from
\yQwenSingleOracleDir\ points (Qwen) to \yMiniSingleOracleDir\ (gpt-4o-mini), both with the default skeleton. On
multi-answer questions it changes little except with gpt-4.1 (gpt-4o-mini \yMiniMultiOracleDir, and
\yMiniMinMultiOracleDir\ with the minimal skeleton; gpt-4.1 \yFourMultiOracleDir). One reading is that a warning about
one passage makes the smaller models more conservative about the legitimate alternative answers too; we did not test
this. Removal does not depend on the model acting on a warning, and it gains in both groups.

\paragraph{A wrong direction (exploratory).} An inverted oracle that names the \emph{correct} side lowers
gpt-4o-mini's accuracy on conflict questions by \aadvDirConf\ points as a directional flag \advDirConfCI, about as
much as a neutral flag, and by \aadvRemConf\ \advRemConfCI\ when the correct passage is removed. Direction is a
strong lever in both directions, which makes it an attack surface (Ethics).

\subsection{What a detector must get right}
\label{sec:grid}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{generated/fig_grid.pdf}
\caption{Synthetic detectors at recall 1: change in one-way strict accuracy (points) against no flags, by pair
precision (rows) and direction accuracy (columns), for the directional flag (top) and removal (bottom). $^*$:

so their rows do not vary with direction accuracy.}
\label{fig:grid}

Figure~\ref{fig:grid} replaces the oracle with synthetic detectors of known quality. Two thresholds appear. First,
\emph{direction accuracy}: at a direction accuracy of 0.5 or 0.75, no cell gains significantly on all questions in
either format. Only perfect direction does (directional flag \gDirOneDOneAll, removal \gRemOneDOneAll\ points at
perfect precision). Second, \emph{precision}, but mainly for removal. On clean questions a cell's prompt does not depend
on direction accuracy, so the false-flag cells reduce to \hFourRemN\ distinct comparisons per format. Removal is
significantly harmful in \hFourRemHarm\ of them, the directional flag in \hFourDirHarm\ (H4a): false flags make the
model delete correct passages, which costs clean questions \agRemHalfDOneClean\ points at precision 0.5 and
\agRemQuDOneClean\ at 0.25, while synthetic false directional flags cost at most two points. The harm tracks the
share of clean questions that lose a correct document (Spearman $\rho=\hFourRho$ over \hFourRhoCells\ distinct
cells, 95\% CI \hFourRhoCI\ from a question-level bootstrap; H4b). Removal hurt clean questions significantly more
than the directional flag in \hFourRemWorse\ of \hFourMatched\ matched cells (H4c; we set no threshold, so we report
the count). Removal is the more powerful and the more fragile interface: its best cell gains twice the directional
flag's on all questions, but below precision 0.75 it is the only format that does net harm.

\subsection{Real detectors (exploratory)}
\label{sec:real}
Table~\ref{tab:detectors} (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}) describes the detectors. The pre-registered F1 ordering (H1) holds only in part. NLI beats
CAAG (\fOneNli\ vs.\ \fOneCaag), but the LLM detector's F1 (\fOneLlm) is not significantly higher than NLI's. Its
direction accuracy is \dirAccLlm\% (95\% CI \dirAccLlmCI, clustered by question). NLI and the LLM detector flag most
clean questions (\cleanFprNli\% and \cleanFprLlm\%); CAAG flags \cleanFprCaag\%.

In the neutral format no real detector helps. CAAG's flags do not beat random flags at the same rate (H5; \dCaagFlagAll\ vs.\ \dRandFlagAll\
points overall). The 90\% CI of the difference, [\tostLo, \tostHi], rules out a gain larger than \tostHi\ points but does not
establish equivalence within $\pm$\tostMargin\ points (TOST).

\emph{Direction helps with a real detector too.} Holding the LLM detector's pairs fixed, the directional flag beats
the neutral flag on conflict questions by \ardMini\ points \rdMiniCI\ ($p\rdMiniP$; registered as exploratory in the
addendum). Against no flags, the directional flag gains \adLlmDirConf\ points on conflict questions (raw
$p=\pLlmDirConfRaw$, not significant after Holm across Table~\ref{tab:main}) and costs \adLlmDirClean\ on clean ones
(\arLlmDirClean\ under the bidirectional metric). Over the 265 labelled questions its net effect is \labLlmDir\
\labLlmDirCI. Removal gains \adLlmRemConf\ on conflict questions and loses \adLlmRemClean\ on clean ones, netting
\labLlmRem\ \labLlmRemCI. With gpt-4.1 as the generator, the directional flag's gain over all 300 questions
(\gptLlmDirAll) is significant after Holm, but the 35 unlabelled questions contribute disproportionately to it (they
gain \agptexclLlmDir\ points); over the labelled questions it is \gptlabLlmDir\ \gptlabLlmDirCI. Real false flags cost
clean questions more than synthetic ones, probably because they concentrate on correct--correct pairs
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). For reference,
MADAM-RAG gains \adMadamAll\ points overall at roughly nine times the prompt tokens. CAAG in its original typed and
scored format behaves the same as in the uniform format (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}).

\subsection{The grid predicts real detectors only roughly}
\label{sec:placement-results}

If the grid only described synthetic detectors, it would be of limited use. Figure~\ref{fig:placement} (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}) tests
whether it predicts real ones (H6). As pre-registered, we bootstrap the whole prediction (cell effects, every detector's
location and the interpolation) over questions, and ask whether the actual effect falls inside the 95\% interval.
Locating detectors by what they do to documents (the share of conflict questions whose misinformation is marked, and
the share of questions that have a correct document marked) puts \hSixDocIn\ of \hSixN\ effects inside; locating them
by pair precision, recall and direction accuracy puts \hSixPairIn\ inside. Document-level errors are smaller on
average (\hSixDocMae\ vs.\ \hSixPairMae\ points), but the paired difference, \hSixMaeDiff\ \hSixMaeDiffCI\ (exact
sign-flip $p=\hSixMaeP$), is not significant over \hSixN\ cases. Both spaces miss the same case, the LLM detector's
removal gain on conflict questions: one correctly directed pair is enough to remove a misinformation document, so the
detector removes the misinformation on \misGoneLlm\% of conflict questions despite its low pair recall. We chose the
document-level axes after seeing the LLM detector on the pilot split. The grid is a useful first estimate of a
detector's effect, but with three detectors we cannot claim that one summary predicts better than the other.

\subsection{Prompt-only baselines and CONFLICTS}
Neither instruction wording changes RAMDocs accuracy significantly after correction (H7). On WikiContradict, v1
(which allows ``genuinely different valid answers'') raises both-sides answers by \awikiInstrOne\ points and v2 (which
asks the model to drop unreliable passages) lowers them by \awikiInstrTwo: each wording builds in one policy, right for
one kind of conflict and wrong for the other. On the 237 automatically scorable CONFLICTS questions (exploratory, one
seed; most have no conflict), no condition moves accuracy
significantly (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}).
