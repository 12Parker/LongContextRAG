\subsection{Does a correct neutral flag help? No, under any answer policy}
\label{sec:neutral}
\input{generated/tab_cb}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{generated/fig_answers.pdf}
\caption{What a flag does to answers on conflict questions (gpt-4o-mini, oracle pairs; share of answers, \%). The
answer policy decides how a neutral flag fails: a model told to give every valid answer adds the misinformation, and
one told there is one answer abstains. Only the directional flag raises the share of correct answers.}
\label{fig:answers}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{RAMDocs.} Flagging exactly the gold contradictions, in the neutral format, lowers gpt-4o-mini's accuracy on
conflict questions by \adOracleFlagConf\ points \dOracleFlagConfCI, from \plainConf\%\ (H2), and gpt-4.1's by
\agptOracleFlagConf\ (H9). It is negative for every generator and skeleton (Table~\ref{tab:generality}), significantly for
gpt-4o-mini, gpt-4.1 and gpt-6-luna. Over all \nNeutralSettings\ settings on both benchmarks, the neutral flag significantly lowers accuracy in
\nNeutralHarm\ and raises it in \nNeutralHelp.

\paragraph{ConflictBank, without the multi-answer policy.} Our default skeleton asks for every valid answer, which
could invite a model to list both sides. On ConflictBank, where every question has one answer, it does: with no flags
only \zpCbMiniConflict\% of gpt-4o-mini's conflict answers are correct, and \zwCbMiniPlain\% include the
misinformation. Told instead that the question has one answer (the \emph{single} prompt, H15), the model reaches
\zpSgMiniConflict\%. The neutral flag still lowers accuracy: by \azSgMiniOracleFlagConflict\ points with gpt-4o-mini,
\azSgFourOracleFlagConflict\ with gpt-4.1 and \azSgLunaOracleFlagConflict\ with gpt-6-luna (all significant; H15,
H19), and by \azSgQwenOracleFlagConflict\ with Qwen2.5-7B and \azSgTerraOracleFlagConflict\ with gpt-6.1-terra (not
significant; Table~\ref{tab:cb}). Only the mechanism changes (Figure~\ref{fig:answers}). Under the multi-answer prompt the flag
makes the model add the misinformation; under the single-answer prompt it makes the model abstain, answering
``unknown'' on \zmSgMiniOracleFlagUnknown\% of conflict questions against \zmSgMiniPlainUnknown\% without flags. On
RAMDocs, under the minimal skeleton, it drops the correct answer instead (gold recall \goldMiniMinPlain\% to
\goldMiniMinOracleFlag\%). The model learns that something is wrong, but not what.

\paragraph{Saying that one side is false makes it worse (H16).} The one-wrong flag tells the model that exactly one
passage of each flagged pair is misinformation, without saying which. It lowers gpt-4o-mini's ConflictBank accuracy by
\azSgMiniOracleFlagOnewrongConflict\ points, and the model abstains on \zmSgMiniOracleFlagOnewrongUnknown\% of conflict
questions. On RAMDocs it changes gpt-4o-mini's accuracy by \zRdMiniOracleFlagOnewrongConflict\ points, and the directional flag
beats it everywhere we tested (H16, H19). The one exception to its uselessness is instructive: on RAMDocs,
gpt-6.1-terra gains \zRdTerraOracleFlagOnewrongConflict\ points from it, nearly as much as from the directional flag
(\zRdTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict). RAMDocs conflict questions usually have several correct passages that agree,
so a strong model told that one side is false can work out which; on ConflictBank, one passage against one, it cannot
(\zSgTerraOracleFlagOnewrongConflict). Knowing that the conflict is misinformation helps only when the passages reveal
which side it is.

\paragraph{Instructions and abstention.} Adding an instruction to drop unreliable passages to the neutral flag only
brings accuracy back to the no-flag level (RAMDocs gpt-4o-mini \zMiniOracleFlagInstructTwoConflict, gpt-4.1 seed 0
\zFourOracleFlagInstructTwoConflict, ConflictBank \zCbMiniOracleFlagInstructTwoConflict\ points; none significant; H13).
Abstaining does halve the share of answers containing the misinformation (\zmSgMiniPlainWrong\% to
\zmSgMiniOracleFlagWrong\%), which a system that prefers abstention to error may value, but the directional flag does
better on both counts (\zmSgMiniOracleFlagDirectionalWrong\% wrong, accuracy \zSgMiniOracleFlagDirectionalConflict).

\paragraph{Genuine conflicts and majorities.} On WikiContradict, where the reference answer is both sides, the same
flag \emph{raises} the share of answers that give both sides by \awikiOracleFlag\ points \wikiOracleFlagCI\ with
gpt-4o-mini and \awikiGptOracleFlag\ with gpt-4.1 (H8; Table~\ref{tab:wiki}). When the misinformation outnumbers the
correct passage three to one, no-flag accuracy on ConflictBank falls to \zpMjMiniConflict\%. The neutral flag then
changes gpt-4o-mini's accuracy by \zMjMiniOracleFlagConflict\ points (H17, n.s.) and gpt-6.1-terra's by
\zMjTerraOracleFlagConflict, but raises gpt-6-luna's by \zMjLunaOracleFlagConflict, the one setting in which it
helps (exploratory).

\subsection{Naming the wrong side is what helps}
\label{sec:direction}
Given the same oracle pairs, the directional flag beats the neutral flag in every setting we ran (H3, H9--H12, H15,
H19) and beats the one-wrong flag (H16: by \hSixteenSgMiniDirOw\ points on ConflictBank and \hSixteenRdMiniDirOw\ on
RAMDocs). On ConflictBank it gains significantly over no flags for every generator (single prompt: gpt-4o-mini
\zSgMiniOracleFlagDirectionalConflict, gpt-4.1 \zSgFourOracleFlagDirectionalConflict, Qwen2.5-7B
\zSgQwenOracleFlagDirectionalConflict, gpt-6-luna \zSgLunaOracleFlagDirectionalConflict, gpt-6.1-terra
\zSgTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ points), and under majority misinformation it gains
\zMjMiniOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ with gpt-4o-mini (H17), \zMjLunaOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ with gpt-6-luna and
\zMjTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ with gpt-6.1-terra (H19). On RAMDocs it gains \adOracleDirConf\ points with gpt-4o-mini, \agptOracleDirConf\ with gpt-4.1,
\azRdLunaOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ with gpt-6-luna and \azRdTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ with
gpt-6.1-terra, but not significantly for Qwen2.5-7B, which largely ignores the header, or for
gpt-4o-mini under the minimal skeleton. These failures concentrate on questions with several gold answers
(Appendix~\ref{app:single}; exploratory). Removing the suspect passage, which needs no cooperation from the model, is
the upper bound and gains in every setting. A wrong direction is as strong a lever the other way: an inverted oracle
that names the correct side costs \aadvDirConf\ points as a directional flag and \aadvRemConf\ with removal on
RAMDocs, and \azCbMiniOracleInvFlagDirectionalConflict\ on ConflictBank (exploratory).

\subsection{With a real detector, direction accuracy is the bottleneck}
\label{sec:real}
\input{generated/tab_baselines}
The gpt-4o-mini detector names the misinformation side of a true contradiction \dirAccLlm\% of the time on RAMDocs,
\cbDirAccLlm\% on ConflictBank and \cbmajDirAccLlm\% (chance) under majority misinformation, and its directional
flag's gain follows. On ConflictBank with the single prompt it gains \zSgMiniLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\ points on
gpt-4o-mini's conflict questions, at a cost of \azSgMiniLlmDirFlagDirectionalClean\ on clean ones; over all
questions the gain (\hFifteenSgMiniLlm) is not significant after Holm within H15 ($p_\text{Holm}=\hFifteenSgMiniLlmP$),
while gpt-6-luna's (\hNineteenSgLunaLlm) is (H19). On RAMDocs it gains \adLlmDirConf\ on conflict
questions (not significant) and \labLlmDir\ \labLlmDirCI\ over labelled ones. Under majority misinformation it gains
\zMjMiniLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict. In the neutral format no real detector helps (Table~\ref{tab:main}); the
heuristic detector we started from does not beat random flags of the same volume (H5).

\paragraph{The detector must beat the generator (exploratory).} The same detector's directional flag helps the three
generators with lower unaided ConflictBank accuracy (gpt-4o-mini \zpSgMiniConflict\%, Qwen2.5-7B \zpSgQwenConflict\%,
gpt-6-luna \zpSgLunaConflict\%; \zSgMiniLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict, \zSgQwenLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\ and
\zSgLunaLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\ points) and hurts the two with higher accuracy (gpt-4.1 \zpSgFourConflict\%,
\zSgFourLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict; gpt-6.1-terra \zpSgTerraConflict\%, \zSgTerraLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict).
A directional flag replaces the generator's judgement with the detector's, so it pays only when the detector's is
better. We noticed this after seeing the results, and five generators with one detector cannot establish it.

\paragraph{Direction is not just memory.} gpt-4o-mini answers only \cbkCbNKnown\ of the \cbkCbNConf\ ConflictBank
conflict questions closed-book; its detector names the right side on \cbkCbKnownDir\% of those and \cbkCbUnknownDir\%
of the rest, so it mostly compares passages rather than recalling the fact.

\paragraph{Against published methods (H14) and a single call (H18).} Table~\ref{tab:baselines} compares the detector
pipeline (one detector and one generator call) with Astute RAG, TrustRAG and a single call that names the suspect
passages before answering. On RAMDocs, Astute RAG and TrustRAG lose valid answers on same-name questions: on clean
questions with several gold answers, gold recall falls from \recMultiPlain\% to \recMultiAst\% and \recMultiTr\%
(exploratory). The directional flag beats both on labelled questions with gpt-4o-mini (by \hFourteenAstMini\ and
\hFourteenTrMini\ points) and TrustRAG with gpt-4.1 (\hFourteenTrFour; seed 0). Their apparent strength on ConflictBank
under the multi-answer prompt comes from that prompt's weak baseline: against the single-answer prompt, Astute
RAG changes conflict-question accuracy by \zSgMiniAstuteConflict\ points and TrustRAG by \zSgMiniTrustragConflict,
while the directional flag gains \zSgMiniLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\ (exploratory; the registered H14 comparison,
under the multi-answer prompt, is a tie, \hFourteenAstCb\ and \hFourteenTrCb). With three misinformation passages,
Astute RAG gains \zMjMiniAstuteConflict\ and TrustRAG \zMjMiniTrustragConflict. With gpt-4o-mini the single
call is as good as the detector pipeline in every setting (H18: \hEighteenRdMiniSelf\ on RAMDocs,
\hEighteenSgMiniSelf\ on ConflictBank, \hEighteenMjMiniSelf\ under majority misinformation; none significant), at half
the calls. With the two reasoning models it backfires: they mark nearly every ConflictBank passage as suspect and
answer ``unknown'', losing \azSgLunaSelfIdentifyClean\ (gpt-6-luna) and \azSgTerraSelfIdentifyClean\ (gpt-6.1-terra)
points on clean questions. A Qwen2.5-7B detector gives the same picture as the gpt-4o-mini one (Appendix~\ref{app:more}).

\subsection{What a detector must get right}
\label{sec:grid}
In synthetic detectors the gain rises with direction accuracy, and precision matters mainly for removal, whose false
flags delete correct passages (H4a); the grid predicts real detectors' effects only roughly (H6). Appendix~\ref{app:grid}
and Figure~\ref{fig:grid} give the cells and tests.
