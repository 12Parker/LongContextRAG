\subsection{A correct neutral flag does not help against misinformation}
\label{sec:neutral}
\input{generated/tab_generality}
Flagging exactly the gold contradictions, in the neutral format, \emph{lowers} gpt-4o-mini's accuracy on conflict
questions by \adOracleFlagConf\ points \dOracleFlagConfCI, from \plainConf\%\ (H2). With gpt-4.1 the drop is
\agptOracleFlagConf\ points (H9). Both hold under either metric. The errors are not refusals: the share of conflict
answers that include the misinformation rises from \wrongPlain\% to \wrongOracleFlag\%, while gold recall barely moves
(\goldPlain\% to \goldOracleFlag\%). The model lists the misinformation \emph{alongside} the correct answer.

\paragraph{Not an artefact of the prompt.} The default skeleton asks for every valid answer, which could invite this
behaviour. Under the minimal skeleton the oracle neutral flag still lowers conflict-question accuracy in all three
generators (Table~\ref{tab:generality}): by \axMiniMinOracleFlagConf\ points with gpt-4o-mini and
\axFourMinOracleFlagConf\ with gpt-4.1 (both significant; H10), and by \axQwenMinOracleFlagConf\ with Qwen2.5-7B (not
significant; \axQwenOracleFlagConf\ under the default skeleton, H11). The mechanism changes. Without the answer
policy, gpt-4o-mini no longer adds the misinformation (\wrongMiniMinPlain\% to \wrongMiniMinOracleFlag\%) but drops
the correct answer (gold recall \goldMiniMinPlain\% to \goldMiniMinOracleFlag\%). A neutral flag says that something
is wrong without saying what, and the model either keeps both sides or loses the right one.

\paragraph{The same flag helps on genuine conflicts.} On WikiContradict, where the reference answer is both sides,
the oracle flag \emph{raises} the share of answers that give both sides by \awikiOracleFlag\ points
\wikiOracleFlagCI\ with gpt-4o-mini and by \awikiGptOracleFlag\ \wikiGptOracleFlagCI\ with gpt-4.1 (H8;
Table~\ref{tab:wiki}). Whether a neutral flag helps depends on whether keeping both sides is right.

\subsection{Acting on direction helps; a directional flag helps when the model follows it}
\label{sec:direction}
Given the same oracle pairs, naming the misinformation side beats the neutral flag on conflict questions in all six
generator--skeleton settings (H3, H9--H11). Against no flags, \emph{removing} the suspect passage gains in every
setting, from \axQwenMinOracleRemConf\ to \agptOracleRemConf\ points, all significant. The directional \emph{flag} is
less consistent. It gains \adOracleDirConf\ points with gpt-4o-mini \dOracleDirConfCI\ and \agptOracleDirConf\ with
gpt-4.1 under the default skeleton, and \axFourMinOracleDirConf\ with gpt-4.1 under the minimal one, but the gain is
not significant for gpt-4o-mini under the minimal skeleton (\xMiniMinOracleDirConf) or for Qwen2.5-7B
(\xQwenOracleDirConf, \xQwenMinOracleDirConf). Qwen largely ignores the header: the share of its answers that include
the misinformation falls only from \wrongQwenPlain\% to \wrongQwenOracleDir\%, against \wrongPlain\% to
\wrongOracleDir\% for gpt-4o-mini. The failures concentrate on questions with several gold answers
(Appendix~\ref{app:single}; exploratory).

\paragraph{A wrong direction (exploratory).} An inverted oracle that names the \emph{correct} side lowers
gpt-4o-mini's conflict-question accuracy by \aadvDirConf\ points as a directional flag \advDirConfCI, about as much
as a neutral flag, and by \aadvRemConf\ \advRemConfCI\ with removal. Direction is a strong lever both ways, which makes
it an attack surface (Ethics).

\subsection{Replication on ConflictBank}
\label{sec:cb}
\input{generated/tab_cb}
ConflictBank had no pilot, and all \nHTwelveTests\ of its registered tests (H12) are supported (Table~\ref{tab:cb}).
On conflict questions the oracle's neutral flag lowers accuracy for all three generators (gpt-4o-mini
\zCbMiniOracleFlagConflict, gpt-4.1 \zCbFourOracleFlagConflict, Qwen2.5-7B \zCbQwenOracleFlagConflict\ points). The
directional flag raises it (\zCbMiniOracleFlagDirectionalConflict, \zCbFourOracleFlagDirectionalConflict,
\zCbQwenOracleFlagDirectionalConflict), now significantly for Qwen too, and removal raises it most
(\zCbMiniOracleRemoveSuspectConflict\ to \zCbFourOracleRemoveSuspectConflict). The mechanism is the same as on
RAMDocs. With gpt-4o-mini, the share of conflict answers that include the misinformation is \zwCbMiniPlain\% with no
flags, \zwCbMiniOracleFlag\% with the neutral flag, \zwCbMiniOracleFlagDirectional\% with the directional flag, and
\zwCbMiniOracleInvFlagDirectional\% when the inverted oracle names the correct passage
(\zCbMiniOracleInvFlagDirectionalConflict\ points). The real LLM detector behaves like a noisier oracle: its neutral
flag costs \azCbMiniLlmDirFlagConflict\ points, and its directional flag gains \zCbMiniLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\
(gpt-4o-mini) and \zCbFourLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\ (gpt-4.1) at most one point on clean questions, though only
\zCbQwenLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\ (not significant) with Qwen. Its direction accuracy is \cbDirAccLlm\%, against
\dirAccLlm\% on RAMDocs. The NLI cross-encoder flags no pair (Appendix~\ref{app:more}).

\subsection{What a detector must get right}
\label{sec:grid}
Synthetic detectors (Appendix~\ref{app:grid}, Figure~\ref{fig:grid}) show two thresholds. Only perfect direction gives
a significant gain over all questions (directional flag \gDirOneDOneAll, removal \gRemOneDOneAll\ points at perfect
precision). Precision matters mainly for removal: false flags make the model delete correct passages, which costs
clean questions \agRemHalfDOneClean\ points at precision 0.5, while false directional flags cost at most two (H4a:
removal is significantly harmful in \hFourRemHarm\ of \hFourRemN\ distinct false-flag cells, the directional flag in
\hFourDirHarm). The grid predicts real detectors' effects only roughly (H6).

\subsection{Real detectors (exploratory)}
\label{sec:real}
The pre-registered F1 ordering (H1; Table~\ref{tab:detectors}) holds only in part: NLI beats CAAG (\fOneNli\ vs.\
\fOneCaag), but the LLM detector (\fOneLlm) is not significantly better than NLI. Its direction accuracy is
\dirAccLlm\% \dirAccLlmCI. NLI and the LLM detector flag most clean questions (\cleanFprNli\% and \cleanFprLlm\%). In
the neutral format no real detector helps; CAAG does not beat random flags of the same volume (H5;
Appendix~\ref{app:more}).

\emph{Direction helps with a real detector too.} Holding the LLM detector's pairs fixed, the directional flag beats
the neutral flag on conflict questions by \ardMini\ points \rdMiniCI\ ($p\rdMiniP$). Against no flags, it gains
\adLlmDirConf\ points on conflict questions (raw $p=\pLlmDirConfRaw$, not significant after Holm) and costs
\adLlmDirClean\ on clean ones; over the labelled questions its net effect is \labLlmDir\ \labLlmDirCI. Removal gains
\adLlmRemConf\ on conflict questions and loses \adLlmRemClean\ on clean ones, netting \labLlmRem\ \labLlmRemCI. With
gpt-4.1, the directional flag's gain over labelled questions is \gptlabLlmDir\ \gptlabLlmDirCI. Real false flags cost
clean questions more than synthetic ones, probably because they concentrate on correct--correct pairs. MADAM-RAG gains
\adMadamAll\ points overall at roughly nine times the prompt tokens.

\subsection{Resolution instructions and published baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}
\input{generated/tab_baselines}
\paragraph{An instruction does not rescue the neutral flag (H13).} Adding the v2 instruction (``ignore unreliable
passages'') to the oracle's neutral flag leaves conflict-question accuracy where it is without flags (RAMDocs
gpt-4o-mini \zMiniOracleFlagInstructTwoConflict, gpt-4.1 \zFourOracleFlagInstructTwoConflict, ConflictBank
\zCbMiniOracleFlagInstructTwoConflict\ points; none significant). With the same instruction, the directional flag
beats it in all three settings (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}), by \hThirteenDirMini, \hThirteenDirFour\ and
\hThirteenDirCb\ points. The instruction removes the neutral flag's harm but adds nothing, because the model still
does not know which side to drop. Given the side, it helps a great deal: the directional flag with the instruction
gains \zCbMiniOracleFlagDirectionalInstructTwoConflict\ points on ConflictBank, close to removal, and
\zFourOracleFlagDirectionalInstructTwoConflict\ with gpt-4.1 on RAMDocs. Instructions alone help in some settings
(gpt-4.1 with v2 on RAMDocs conflict questions, \gptInstrTwoConf; v1 on WikiContradict, \awikiInstrOne), but each
wording builds in one policy: v2 lowers both-sides answers on WikiContradict by \awikiInstrTwo\ points, and neither
changes gpt-4o-mini's RAMDocs accuracy significantly (H7).

\paragraph{Against published methods (H14).} Astute RAG and TrustRAG resolve conflicts inside the generator, with two
and three calls. On RAMDocs both lose accuracy on clean questions (\zMiniAstuteMultiClean\ and
\zMiniTrustragMultiClean\ points with gpt-4o-mini, multi variants). They lose valid answers: on clean questions with several gold
answers, gold recall falls from \recMultiPlain\% to \recMultiAst\% (Astute RAG) and \recMultiTr\% (TrustRAG), against \recSinglePlain\% to \recSingleAst\% and
\recSingleTr\% with one gold answer (exploratory). Consolidating to the best-supported answer is the wrong policy when passages
describe different same-name entities. On labelled RAMDocs questions, a single call with the LLM detector's directional
flag beats Astute RAG by \hFourteenAstMini\ points and TrustRAG by \hFourteenTrMini\ with gpt-4o-mini, and with
gpt-4.1 beats TrustRAG by \hFourteenTrFour\ and Astute RAG by \hFourteenAstFour\ (not significant). On ConflictBank,
which has one answer per question, the baselines gain (Astute RAG \zCbMiniAstuteConflict, TrustRAG
\zCbMiniTrustragConflict\ points on conflict questions) and the directional flag ties both (\hFourteenAstCb,
\hFourteenTrCb; not significant). Of the methods we tried with a real detector, only the directional flag does no significant harm on either
benchmark or question type while gaining on ConflictBank, and it costs one generator call. A Qwen2.5-7B detector gives the same picture (Appendix~\ref{app:more}), and on CONFLICTS no
condition moves accuracy significantly (Table~\ref{tab:conflicts}).
