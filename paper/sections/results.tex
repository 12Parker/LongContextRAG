\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Sections~\ref{sec:neutral}--\ref{sec:real} each state one claim, its status and its scope. All numbers use the
one-way matcher unless stated. Appendix~\ref{app:extra} gives every condition and, in Table~\ref{tab:claims}, each
claim's status; Appendix~\ref{app:hyp} lists every registered test.

\subsection{Claim 1: a neutral flag shifts policy}
\label{sec:neutral}

A neutral flag on the gold contradictions says that two passages disagree, not which one is wrong. Under RAMDocs'
multi-answer prompt it changes gpt-4o-mini's conflict-question accuracy by \dOracleFlagConf\ points
\dOracleFlagConfCI\ from \plainConf\% (H2, registered before any confirmatory data). Where answers go depends on the
prompt's policy (Figure~\ref{fig:riskcov}).

\paragraph{One answer asked for: abstention.}
On ConflictBank's one-answer prompt the flag raises abstention by \ahTwentyOneSgMiniFlagAbstain\ points for
gpt-4o-mini and \ahTwentyOneSgLunaFlagAbstain\ for gpt-6-luna (H21), and also for gpt-4.1 and Qwen2.5-7B
(exploratory). gpt-4o-mini loses misinformed and correct answers alike: \trSgMiniNeutralMisinfoAbstain\% of its
misinformed and \trSgMiniNeutralCorrectAbstain\% of its correct answers become abstentions. Its selective accuracy
shows no detectable change (\selRSgMiniFlagA\% to \selRSgMiniFlagB\%, \selRSgMiniFlag\ \selRSgMiniFlagCI) under the
ratio estimand of \prs{15.4.5}, which replaced the registered per-question mean after the data were seen (H21:
\hTwentyOneSgMiniFlagSelective\ \hTwentyOneSgMiniFlagSelectiveCI). Strict accuracy, which counts abstention as
wrong, falls by \azSgMiniOracleFlagConflict\ points (H15): the flag lowers coverage without lowering risk. Across the
\rcSingleN\ one-answer settings (CIs excluding zero, unadjusted), abstention rises in \rcSingleAbstUp\ and falls in
\rcSingleAbstDown, both on RAGuard, where few flagged pairs contradict; selective accuracy rises in \rcSingleSelUp\
and falls in \rcSingleSelDown, and strict accuracy rises in \rcSingleStrictUp. These gains come mostly from the
majority variant, where misinformation passages outnumber the correct one and the added abstentions fall mostly on
misinformed answers, and from RAGuard. Three generators barely move: abstention goes from
\zmSgHaikuPlainOutAbstain\% to \zmSgHaikuOracleFlagOutAbstain\% for Claude Haiku, \zmSgSonnetPlainOutAbstain\% to
\zmSgSonnetOracleFlagOutAbstain\% for Claude Sonnet and \zmSgTerraPlainOutAbstain\% to
\zmSgTerraOracleFlagOutAbstain\% for gpt-6.1-terra (H21 tests Haiku and terra; terra's CI excludes zero, but it is
not significant under Holm). Under forced choice its only significant accuracy effect is Claude Haiku's
\hTwentyFcHaikuFlag\ (H20).

\paragraph{Every valid answer asked for: misinformation.}
Under the multi-answer prompt coverage barely moves and the model lists both sides: across the \rcMultiN\ settings
the misinformation share rises in \rcMultiMisUp\ and selective accuracy falls in \rcMultiSelDown\ (CIs excluding
zero, unadjusted). Claude Haiku on RAMDocs moves the other way: its misinformation share falls by
\aoneWayBoldLostDiff\ points (significant under the bidirectional matcher only, R12). The minimal skeleton, which
states no policy, raises abstention in \rcMinimalAbstUp\ of its \rcMinimalN\ settings (same criterion). On
WikiContradict, where both sides are valid and a correct answer names both, the same compliance is rewarded: the flag
raises accuracy by \awikiOracleFlag\ points (H8).

\paragraph{Cost of a wrong answer.}
If an answer containing the misinformation costs $c$ and a correct one gains 1, gpt-4o-mini's one-answer utility
change turns positive between $c=1$ and $c=2$ (\utSgMiniNeutralOne\ and \utSgMiniNeutralTwo) and is significantly
positive only at $c=4$ (\utSgMiniNeutralFour); under the multi-answer prompt it is negative at every $c$
(Table~\ref{tab:utility}). \emph{Status:} H2 and H8 are registered in tier (i), H15, H20 and H21 in tier (ii); the
risk--coverage counts and utility are exploratory, and pooled summaries describe only these \rcAllN\ settings
(Table~\ref{tab:pooled}). The outside-family judge moves sampled contrasts by \semDidMin\ to \semDidMax\ points
(Appendix~\ref{app:semantic}), so claim 1 is about direction, not size.

\subsection{Claim 2: deference to directional flags}
\label{sec:direction}

\paragraph{An oracle's direction helps.}
Naming the labelled false side on the gold pairs beats the neutral and one-wrong flags in every registered
comparison on RAMDocs and ConflictBank (Table~\ref{tab:claims}). Over no flag it gains
\azSgSonnetOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ to \azSgLunaOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ points on ConflictBank's
one-answer prompt for every generator, and on RAMDocs \adOracleDirConf\ for gpt-4o-mini and up to
\azRdTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ for gpt-6.1-terra, though not significantly for Qwen2.5-7B or
minimal-prompt gpt-4o-mini (H10).

\paragraph{Randomised direction (H31).}
Each of the 300 ConflictBank conflict questions was flagged on its true pair, naming the false side for a random
\nRandRight\ and the correct side for the other \nRandWrong. Because a coin flip assigns the direction, the effects
measure deference to the flag, whatever cues the passages carry. A right flag raises accuracy by \hThirtyOneAMin\ to
\hThirtyOneAMax\ points ($\Delta_R$) and a wrong flag lowers it by \ahThirtyOneBMax\ to \ahThirtyOneBMin\ ($\Delta_W$;
Table~\ref{tab:deference}). At an even mix the net effect is significantly negative for \nHThirtyOneCSig\ of
\nHThirtyOneCTests\ generators; for gpt-4o-mini it is \hThirtyOneCMini\ \hThirtyOneCMiniCI\ (H31c). Three of the four
follow wrong flags about as often as right ones; gpt-6.1-terra, the most accurate of the four, follows
\rfolWrongTerra\% of wrong flags against \rfolRightTerra\% of right ones. They follow wrong flags although the flagged
passage has the style a simple classifier reads as correct; their no-flag accuracy is far below that classifier's, so
they do not fully use the cue.

\paragraph{Break-even direction precision.}
Randomised flags on true pairs with direction precision $p$ change accuracy by $p\Delta_R+(1-p)\Delta_W$, positive
only above $p^*$ (Table~\ref{tab:terms}). $p^*$ rises with generator accuracy, from \beMin\ for gpt-4o-mini to
\beMax\ for gpt-6.1-terra (exploratory; \terraDrawAltBe\ with terra's other no-flag draw, R7): an accurate generator
has more correct answers for a wrong flag to overturn, and terra also follows wrong flags more often. Randomisation
keeps the detector's cues out of $\Delta_R$ and $\Delta_W$, but not the generator's: its no-flag accuracy, which sets
how much a wrong flag can cost, is itself reached on passages whose false side is legible from style. A RAMDocs
grid registered before any confirmatory data is consistent with this: at perfect pair precision and recall,
synthetic detectors change gpt-4o-mini's conflict accuracy by \gDirOneDHalfConf, \gDirOneDTquConf\ and
\gDirOneDOneConf\ points at $d=0.5$, 0.75 and 1, significantly only at $d=1$ (Appendix~\ref{app:grid}).

\paragraph{Scope.}
On ConflictBank the real detectors' direction precision is \ssPrecMin--\ssPrecMax\% (R3). In the \prs{11.3} split
(registered as exploratory; both definitions agree for this detector), the gpt-4o-mini detector names the false side
more often on facts its model knows closed-book (\cbkCbKnownDir\% on \cbkCbKnownN) than on the \cbkCbUnknownN\ it
does not (\cbkCbUnknownDir\% on the \priorSsMiniUnknownN\ it flags). Every detector with a closed-book run shows this
gap, by \priorSsDiffMin\ to \priorSsDiffMax\ points, with a CI above zero for \priorSsDiffPos\ of \priorN\ (R2). On
RAMDocs, where several documents can agree, no gap is visible, on only \cbkRdKnownN\ known facts
(Appendix~\ref{app:rev11}). With three misinformation passages per question, the gpt-4o-mini detector's direction
precision falls from \cbDirAccLlm\% to \cbmajDirAccLlm\% (\prs{11.3}; `both' counted as a miss), so its verdict moves
with passage counts. Conditioning follow rates on the generator's prior is uninformative here: known facts are rare, and
the pooled odds ratio of following a wrong flag on one is \prFolOrW\ \prFolOrWCI\ (R4). \emph{Status:} the H31 effects are registered (tier ii); follow rates are
registered descriptives without a test (\prs{16.3}); $p^*$ is exploratory.

\subsection{Claim 3: a frozen account predicts}
\label{sec:real}

Equation~\eqref{eq:account} was written after the crossed cells on our 300 ConflictBank questions had been seen
(Table~\ref{tab:cells}). Frozen with each generator's H31 follow rates, it was applied to ConflictBank-fresh: five
detectors crossed with the four H31 generators under the same one-answer prompt (\frN\ cells); Qwen2.5-7B is new as a
detector, and most other pairs had been crossed before, on other questions. Predictions were committed after the
detections and no-flag answers existed and before any flagged answer did (H32--H36, prospective;
Table~\ref{tab:fresh}).

\paragraph{Conflict questions.}
H32 required a mean absolute error of at most 3 points, a calibration slope whose CI excludes 0 and includes 1, and
a lower error than each generator's mean crossed-cell effect (\frMaeGen) and follow rates of 1 (\frMaeOnes). All
hold: the error is \frMae\ points \frMaeCI\ (predicting zero: \frMaeZero), the slope \frSlope\ \frSlopeCI. The criteria were set knowing the retrospective fit, and two baselines without a criterion come
close (R6, exploratory): replacing each per-question term by its cell mean scores \frMaeMarg\ (difference \acctMargDiff\
\acctMargDiffCI), and one pair of rates for all four generators scores \acctCommonMae\ (difference \acctCommonDiff\
\acctCommonDiffCI). Neither is detectably worse, so most of the predictive power comes from cell-level bookkeeping
and follow rates of the right size, not from which questions are flagged or from each generator's own rates. Within
generators, predicted and observed effects correlate at \frRWithin\ (H34). The sign is right in all \frBigN\ cells
predicted at least 3 points from zero, but only \acctHarmN\ of them are predicted harms, both with gpt-6.1-terra
generating: with the Qwen2.5-7B detector it was predicted to lose \aacctHarmTerraQwenPred\ points and lost
\aacctHarmTerraQwenObs; with the gpt-4o-mini detector, \aacctHarmTerraMiniPred\ and \aacctHarmTerraMiniObs. H33 also
required each predicted harm to be significant; only the first is, so H33 fails. A fresh randomised run gives
gpt-4o-mini follow rates of \frFollowR\% and \frFollowW\%, inside the frozen intervals (H36).

\paragraph{Where it fails.}
On clean questions (H35) the account predicts \frCleanPred\ points on average against \frCleanObs\ observed. Cells
whose detector flags no clean question are zero by construction; over the \acctCleanNFlag\ that flag some, the mean
error is \acctCleanMaeFlag\ points \acctCleanMaeFlagCI\ (R6). $f_W$ was measured where following a wrong flag means
adopting the misinformation; on a clean question it can only mean dropping a correct answer, and it does not
transfer. On HealthContradict the account overstates harm for all three generators (H37d; Table~\ref{tab:hc}):
gpt-4o-mini \hcLlmPredMini\ predicted against \hcLlmMini\ observed, gpt-6-luna \hcLlmPredLuna\ against \hcLlmLuna,
Claude Haiku \hcLlmPredHaiku\ against \hcLlmHaiku. Label noise could explain this (a reversed label makes a ``wrong'' flag
right), which we cannot test before humans label the pages (\prs{20.3}). The generators'
higher prior there (closed-book above plain accuracy) is another candidate.

\subsection{What does not hold, or is not settled}
\label{sec:open}

\paragraph{Answer keys.}
If a flag conveys only the answer it implies, a key should do as well. Under the outside-family judge the flag leads
by \jdTwentyThreeJ\ \jdTwentyThreeJCI\ points over the answer key and \jdThirtyJ\ \jdThirtyJCI\ over the verdict key
(H38), so neither equivalence holds within $\pm$3. Under the string matcher no registered comparison finds the
oracle flag significantly better than an answer key (H23) or the LLM flag better than its verdict key (H30), but only
\hTwentyThreeEqN\ of the \hTwentyThreeK\ H23 TOSTs show equivalence; pooled (unregistered), the flag's advantage is
\PoolTwentyThree\ \PoolTwentyThreeCI\ and \PoolThirtyA\ \PoolThirtyACI\ (seed by seed: R11), equivalent within
$\pm$3 under the matcher only, as \prs{19.2} requires us to state. Whether a key conveys what the flag conveys stays unresolved until humans
decide between the scorers.

\paragraph{Stress tests.}
On HealthContradict each item has one page per yes/no side, so an oracle flag on the misinformation page effectively
states the answer; its gain (\hcDirMini\ to \hcDirHaiku\ points, H37a) measures deference to the released labels. On
RAGuard the answer key beats the oracle flag for \rgKeyAbove\ of \rgKeyGens\ generators, by \rgKeyKeyFlagHaiku\ to
\rgKeyKeyFlagLuna\ points, and falls below it for gpt-4o-mini (\rgKeyKeyFlagMini; R14; CIs exclude zero,
unadjusted): there the flag conveys less than the answer it implies, and all four generators run closed-book
(\rgCbMin--\rgCbMax\%) do better without the posts. CONFLICTS shows no significant change.

\paragraph{Genuine conflicts.}
Where both sides are valid, a directional flag and removal discard valid answers (H28; Table~\ref{tab:wiki}). On
WikiContradict the LLM directional flag lowers the share of answers giving both sides by \ahTwentyEightWcMiniDir\ to
\ahTwentyEightWcLunaDir\ points; on RAMDocs' same-name questions removal costs \ahTwentyEightAmbMiniRem\ to
\ahTwentyEightAmbLunaRem.

\paragraph{Prevalence.}
A real detector also flags clean questions, so its net effect depends on how common conflicts are. For the
gpt-4o-mini detector's directional flag, \pvBeN\ of \pvN\ settings have a break-even conflict share above which it
helps (\pvBeMin\% to \pvBeMax\%, median \pvBeMedian\%); in \pvHelpsAll\ it helps and in \pvHurtsAll\ it hurts at
every share (R18). These shares depend on how each benchmark builds its clean questions
(Table~\ref{tab:prev11}), so no single conflict share decides.
