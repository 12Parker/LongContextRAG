\subsection{What a neutral flag does depends on the answer policy}
\label{sec:neutral}
\input{generated/tab_risk}

Flagging exactly the gold contradictions in the neutral format lowers gpt-4o-mini's RAMDocs conflict-question accuracy
by \adOracleFlagConf\ points \dOracleFlagConfCI\ from \plainConf\% (H2), and gpt-4.1's by \agptOracleFlagConf\ (H9).
How depends on what the prompt asks for (Table~\ref{tab:risk}).

\paragraph{Asked for every valid answer, the model adds the misinformation.} Under RAMDocs' default prompt, which asks
for every valid answer, the neutral flag raises the share of gpt-4o-mini's
answers that contain the misinformation from \zmRdMiniPlainOutMisinfo\% to \zmRdMiniOracleFlagOutMisinfo\% (and on
ConflictBank under the same prompt from \zmCbMiniPlainOutMisinfo\% to \zmCbMiniOracleFlagOutMisinfo\%): told that two
passages conflict, it reports both sides. Claude Haiku does not (\zmRdHaikuPlainOutMisinfo\% to
\zmRdHaikuOracleFlagOutMisinfo\%).

\paragraph{Asked for one answer, it abstains.} With a prompt stating that the question has one answer, the neutral
flag costs gpt-4o-mini \azSgMiniOracleFlagConflict\ points on ConflictBank, gpt-4.1 \azSgFourOracleFlagConflict\ and
gpt-6-luna \azSgLunaOracleFlagConflict\ (H15, H19), and changes accuracy insignificantly for gpt-6.1-terra,
Qwen2.5-7B, either Claude model (H25) and every generator on RAGuard (H26). Of gpt-4o-mini's answers that contained the
misinformation, \trSgMiniNeutralMisinfoAbstain\% abstain with the flag and \trSgMiniNeutralMisinfoCorrect\% become
correct (against \trNullSgMiniMisinfoCorrect\% between two unflagged seeds), and \trSgMiniNeutralCorrectAbstain\% of
its correct answers also abstain (Table~\ref{tab:utility}). Accuracy on the answers it still gives moves by
\selNeuMin\ to \selNeuMax\ points across the eight ConflictBank settings of H21, significantly in \nSelNeuSig;
equivalence within $\pm$3 points holds in \nSelNeuEq. When ``unknown'' is not allowed (H20), the flag has no
significant effect for gpt-4o-mini (\hTwentyFcMiniFlag), gpt-6-luna (\hTwentyFcLunaFlag) or gpt-6.1-terra
(\hTwentyFcTerraFlag), equivalence not established, and a small one for Claude Haiku (\hTwentyFcHaikuFlag).

\paragraph{Across settings.} In a multilevel model over the \neuMlK\ settings (Table~\ref{tab:pooled}; exploratory),
the pooled effect is \neuMlMu\ points \neuMlCI\ and a new setting in this design space would fall in \neuMlPI. It is
\neuPolMulti\ under multi-answer prompts, \neuPolSingle\ under one-answer prompts and \neuPolForced\ under forced
choice; Claude (\neuMlAnthropic) and forced choice (\neuMlForced) shift it up. Counting abstention as costless and a
misinformed answer as $c$ wrong ones, the flag lowers utility significantly in \nUtilSixNegZero\ of
\nUtilSixSettings\ settings at $c=0$ and raises it in \nUtilSixPosZero; at $c=4$ it raises it in
\nUtilSixPosFour\ and lowers it in \nUtilSixNegFour\ (gpt-4o-mini on one-answer ConflictBank: from
\usSgMiniNeutralZero\ to \usSgMiniNeutralFour). On WikiContradict, where both sides are valid, listing both is
correct, and the flag raises accuracy (H8: \wikiOracleFlag).

\subsection{Naming the false side: what an oracle can deliver}
\label{sec:direction}
With the same oracle pairs, the directional flag beats the neutral and one-wrong flags in every registered comparison
but one (H3, H9--H12, H15, H16, H19, H25, H26; gpt-6.1-terra on RAGuard is not significant). On ConflictBank it gains
\azSgTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ to \azSgLunaOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ points for the OpenAI generators
and \azSgSonnetOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ to \azSgHaikuOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ for Claude, under forced
choice (H20: \hTwentyFcTerraDir\ to \hTwentyFcLunaDir), under majority misinformation
(\zMjMiniOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ to \zMjTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict) and on RAGuard (H26:
\hTwentySixRgTerraDir\ to \hTwentySixRgSonnetDir). On RAMDocs it gains \adOracleDirConf\ points with gpt-4o-mini and
up to \azRdTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ with gpt-6.1-terra, but not significantly for Qwen2.5-7B.

These rows are a ceiling, not a detector result. Telling the model ``the answer $X$ is false'', with no passage
marked, does nearly as well: no registered comparison differs significantly (H23), the flag's advantage ranges from
\keyDiffMin\ to \keyDiffMax\ points, and equivalence within $\pm$3 holds in \nTostTwentyThreeEq\ of
\nTostTwentyThreeTests; for gpt-4o-mini on ConflictBank the flag is ahead (90\% CI \tostKeySgMini). An inverted oracle
that names the correct side costs \aadvDirConf\ points on RAMDocs (Table~\ref{tab:inverted}). Saying only that one
side is false helps when the model must answer: the one-wrong flag lowers gpt-4o-mini's one-answer ConflictBank
accuracy by \azSgMiniOracleFlagOnewrongConflict\ points (H16) but raises every generator's under forced choice (H20:
\hTwentyFcHaikuOw\ to \hTwentyFcLunaOw). Removing the pair structure has no consistent effect (H22).

\subsection{With a real detector, its wrong flags decide}
\label{sec:real}
\paragraph{Registered results.} With the gpt-4o-mini LLM detector, the directional flag significantly raises
accuracy over all ConflictBank questions for \nRealSgPos\ of \nRealSgTests\ generators (gpt-6-luna,
\zSgLunaLlmDirFlagDirectionalLabelled) and significantly lowers it for \nRealSgNeg\ (gpt-6.1-terra,
\zSgTerraLlmDirFlagDirectionalLabelled; H15, H19, H25). It helps all \nRealMjTests\ generators under majority
misinformation (\zMjMiniLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\ to \zMjTerraLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict), and on RAGuard the
two Claude models (H26: \hTwentySixRgHaikuLlm, \hTwentySixRgSonnetLlm) but no OpenAI one.

\paragraph{A wrong flag costs more than a right one gains.} In a randomised test (H31), every ConflictBank conflict
question got a flag on its true contradiction, naming the false side on a random \nRandRight\ questions and the
correct side on the other \nRandWrong. A right flag raises accuracy by \hThirtyOneAMin\ to \hThirtyOneAMax\ points and
a wrong one lowers it by \ahThirtyOneBMax\ to \ahThirtyOneBMin\ (Figure~\ref{fig:reliability}, left). The net is
significantly negative for \nHThirtyOneCSig\ of \nHThirtyOneCTests\ generators, and more so the more accurate the
generator: \hThirtyOneCLuna\ for gpt-6-luna, \hThirtyOneCHaiku\ for Claude Haiku, \hThirtyOneCTerra\ for gpt-6.1-terra,
and \hThirtyOneCMini\ (n.s.) for gpt-4o-mini. Three of the four generators follow right and wrong flags at
similar rates (gpt-4o-mini \rfolRightMini\% and \rfolWrongMini\%, gpt-6-luna \rfolRightLuna\% and \rfolWrongLuna\%, Claude
Haiku \rfolRightHaiku\% and \rfolWrongHaiku\%; gpt-6.1-terra \rfolRightTerra\% and \rfolWrongTerra\%;
Table~\ref{tab:h24redo}); a wrong flag costs more mainly because an accurate generator has more correct answers to
lose.

\paragraph{Real detectors, crossed with generators.} H24 crossed six detectors with six generators in
\hTwentyFourCells\ cells. As registered, the gain rises with the detector's hit rate (it flags the gold pair and names
its false side) minus the generator's no-flag accuracy ($\rho=\hTwentyFourRho$, $p\hTwentyFourP$; \hRedoPostCells\
cells added after registration: $\rho=\hRedoPostRho$). But the generator's accuracy enters both sides, and most of the
correlation comes from it: leaving out one detector at a time, the registered gap predicts held-out cells worse than
their mean ($R^2=\hRedoCvGap$; generator accuracy alone \hRedoCvG). Within generators, the detector's precision (of its
flags on the gold pair, the share naming the false side) and its recall both matter ($p=\recPPerm$ and $\recRPerm$,
exact permutation over the six detectors; exploratory), but no detector-level summary predicts held-out detectors
(Table~\ref{tab:h24redo}). The gpt-4o-mini detector, the least precise (\precMini\% against
\precOtherMin--\precOtherMax\%), flags almost every question and marks a correct passage on \detWrongDirMini\% of
them; it helps the weaker generators and lowers the accuracy of the four most accurate, significantly for
gpt-6.1-terra (H19). gpt-6.1-terra as a detector flags \detAnyFlagTerra\% of questions, marks a correct passage on
\detWrongDirTerra\%, and lowers no generator's accuracy.

\paragraph{What the detector contributes is its verdict.} Passing the detector's judgement as an answer key (``the
answer $X$ is false'' for the answer its suspect passage supports, no passage marked) does as well as the directional
flag in every cell we ran (H30: \hThirtyAMin\ to \hThirtyAMax\ points, none significant; equivalence within
$\pm$3 shown in \nHThirtyTostEq\ of \nHThirtyATests), and beats the detector model's answer as a hint in
\nHThirtyBPos. Giving the generator the
detector model's own answer instead does worse than the flag in \nHTwentySevenAPos\ of \nHTwentySevenATests\ cells and
never better (H27), and lowers the accuracy of the stronger generators even when the hinted answer is better than
their own. Using the detector model as the generator beats the cell in only \nHTwentySevenCNeg,
each a much stronger model serving a weaker one.

\paragraph{Artefacts and genuine conflicts.} Reversing the passage order or paraphrasing every passage changes the
detectors' hit rate by \hTwentyNineLunaRev\ to \hTwentyNineMiniPara\ points from the original (gpt-4o-mini
\hTwentyNineMiniRevOrig\%, gpt-6-luna \hTwentyNineLunaRevOrig\%; H29), none significant, though only one of four
changes is shown to be within $\pm$5 points. Where both sides are valid, direction does damage (H28): on WikiContradict
the LLM directional flag lowers the share of answers giving both sides by \ahTwentyEightWcMiniDir\ points for
gpt-4o-mini, \ahTwentyEightWcLunaDir\ for gpt-6-luna and \ahTwentyEightWcHaikuDir\ for Claude Haiku, and on the
\hTwentyEightAmbMiniN\ RAMDocs same-name questions removal costs every generator \ahTwentyEightAmbMiniRem\ to
\ahTwentyEightAmbLunaRem\ points.

\paragraph{Baselines.} Astute RAG \citep{wang2025astute} and TrustRAG \citep{zhou2025trustrag} lose same-name answers
on RAMDocs (H14). On ConflictBank the registered H14 compared our pipeline under the multi-answer prompt with the
baselines' own one-answer prompts; under the matched one-answer prompt it beats them by \hFourteenMatchedAst\ and
\hFourteenMatchedTr\ points (exploratory). A single call that names suspect passages before answering does not differ
significantly from the pipeline (H18; Table~\ref{tab:baselines}).
