\subsection{What a neutral flag does depends on the answer policy}
\label{sec:neutral}
\input{generated/tab_risk}

Flagging exactly the gold contradictions in the neutral format lowers gpt-4o-mini's RAMDocs conflict-question accuracy
by \adOracleFlagConf\ points \dOracleFlagConfCI\ from \plainConf\% (H2), and gpt-4.1's by \agptOracleFlagConf\ (H9).
How depends on what the prompt asks for (Table~\ref{tab:risk}).

\paragraph{Asked for every valid answer, the model adds the misinformation.} Under RAMDocs' default prompt, which asks
for every valid answer, the neutral flag raises the share of gpt-4o-mini's
answers that contain the misinformation from \zmRdMiniPlainWrong\% to \zmRdMiniOracleFlagWrong\% (and on
ConflictBank under the same prompt from \zmCbMiniPlainWrong\% to \zmCbMiniOracleFlagWrong\%): told that two
passages conflict, it reports both sides. Every OpenAI and Qwen generator moves the same way (e.g.\ gpt-4.1 on
ConflictBank \zmCbFourPlainWrong\% to \zmCbFourOracleFlagWrong\%); both Claude models move the other way (Haiku on
RAMDocs \zmRdHaikuPlainWrong\% to \zmRdHaikuOracleFlagWrong\%).

\paragraph{Asked for one answer, it abstains.} With a prompt stating that the question has one answer, the neutral
flag costs gpt-4o-mini \azSgMiniOracleFlagConflict\ points on ConflictBank, gpt-4.1 \azSgFourOracleFlagConflict\ and
gpt-6-luna \azSgLunaOracleFlagConflict\ (H15, H19), and changes accuracy insignificantly for gpt-6.1-terra,
Qwen2.5-7B, either Claude model (H25) and every generator on RAGuard (H26). Of gpt-4o-mini's misinformed answers, \trSgMiniNeutralMisinfoAbstain\% abstain with the flag and
\trSgMiniNeutralMisinfoCorrect\% become correct (\trNullSgMiniMisinfoCorrect\% between two unflagged seeds), and
\trSgMiniNeutralCorrectAbstain\% of its correct answers also abstain; accuracy on the answers it still gives moves by
\selNeuMin\ to \selNeuMax\ points (H21). When ``unknown'' is not allowed (H20), the flag has no
significant effect for gpt-4o-mini (\hTwentyFcMiniFlag), gpt-6-luna (\hTwentyFcLunaFlag) or gpt-6.1-terra
(\hTwentyFcTerraFlag), equivalence not established, and a small one for Claude Haiku (\hTwentyFcHaikuFlag).

\paragraph{Across settings.} In a multilevel model over the \neuMlK\ settings (Table~\ref{tab:pooled}; exploratory),
the pooled effect is \neuMlMu\ points \neuMlCI\ and a new setting in this design space would fall in \neuMlPI. It is
\neuPolMinimal\ \neuPolMinimalCI\ under the minimal prompt, which states no answer policy, \neuPolMulti\ under
multi-answer prompts, \neuPolSingle\ under one-answer prompts and \neuPolForced\ under forced choice; Claude
(\neuMlAnthropic) shifts it up about as much as forced choice (\neuMlForced). The policy and family codings were
chosen after seeing data. If abstention is costless and a misinformed answer
costs $c$ wrong ones, the flag lowers utility significantly in \nUtilSixNegZero\ of \nUtilSixSettings\ settings at
$c=0$, and raises it in \nUtilSixPosFour\ at $c=4$ (Table~\ref{tab:utility}). On WikiContradict, where both sides are valid, listing both is
correct, and the flag raises accuracy (H8: \wikiOracleFlag).

\subsection{Naming the false side: what an oracle can deliver}
\label{sec:direction}
With the same oracle pairs, the directional flag beats the neutral and one-wrong flags in every registered comparison
but one (H3, H9--H12, H15, H16, H19, H25, H26; gpt-6.1-terra on RAGuard is not significant). On ConflictBank it gains
\azSgTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ to \azSgLunaOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ points for the OpenAI generators
and \azSgSonnetOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ to \azSgHaikuOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ for Claude, under forced
choice (H20: \hTwentyFcTerraDir\ to \hTwentyFcLunaDir), under majority misinformation
(\zMjMiniOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ to \zMjTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict) and on RAGuard (H26:
\hTwentySixRgTerraDir\ to \hTwentySixRgSonnetDir). On RAMDocs it gains \adOracleDirConf\ points with gpt-4o-mini and
up to \azRdTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ with gpt-6.1-terra, but not significantly for Qwen2.5-7B or for
gpt-4o-mini under the minimal prompt (H10). The ConflictBank ranges are for the one-answer prompt.

These rows are a ceiling, not a detector result. Telling the model ``the answer $X$ is false'', with no passage
marked, does as well: no registered comparison differs significantly (H23; \keyDiffMin\ to \keyDiffMax\ points), and
pooled over the \PoolTwentyThreeK\ comparisons, with a bootstrap that resamples each benchmark's questions jointly
across them, the flag's advantage is \PoolTwentyThree\ points \PoolTwentyThreeCI, equivalent within $\pm$3 (90\% CI
\PoolTwentyThreeCINinety; exploratory). An inverted oracle
that names the correct side costs \aadvDirConf\ points on RAMDocs (Table~\ref{tab:inverted}). Saying only that one
side is false helps when the model must answer: the one-wrong flag lowers gpt-4o-mini's one-answer ConflictBank
accuracy by \azSgMiniOracleFlagOnewrongConflict\ points (H16) but raises every generator's under forced choice (H20:
\hTwentyFcHaikuOw\ to \hTwentyFcLunaOw).

\subsection{With a real detector, its wrong flags decide}
\label{sec:real}
\paragraph{Registered results.} With the gpt-4o-mini LLM detector, the directional flag significantly raises
accuracy over all ConflictBank questions for \nRealSgPos\ of \nRealSgTests\ generators (gpt-6-luna,
\zSgLunaLlmDirFlagDirectionalLabelled) and significantly lowers it for \nRealSgNeg\ (gpt-6.1-terra,
\zSgTerraLlmDirFlagDirectionalLabelled; H15, H19, H25). It helps all \nRealMjTests\ generators under majority
misinformation (\zMjMiniLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\ to \zMjTerraLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict), and on RAGuard the
two Claude models (H26: \hTwentySixRgHaikuLlm, \hTwentySixRgSonnetLlm) but no OpenAI one.

\paragraph{A wrong flag costs more than a right one gains.} In a randomised test (H31), every ConflictBank conflict
question got a flag on its true contradiction, naming the false side on a random \nRandRight\ questions and the
correct side on the other \nRandWrong. A right flag raises accuracy by \hThirtyOneAMin\ to \hThirtyOneAMax\ points and
a wrong one lowers it by \ahThirtyOneBMax\ to \ahThirtyOneBMin\ (Figure~\ref{fig:reliability}, left). At this split
the net is significantly negative for \nHThirtyOneCSig\ of \nHThirtyOneCTests\ generators. Each generator therefore has
a break-even direction precision, $-W/(R-W)$ for right- and wrong-flag effects $R$ and $W$, below which a flag on the
true pair does net harm: \beMini\ \beMiniCI\ for gpt-4o-mini, \beLuna\ for gpt-6-luna, \beHaiku\ for Claude Haiku and
\beTerra\ \beTerraCI\ for gpt-6.1-terra (Table~\ref{tab:breakeven}; exploratory). Generators follow right and wrong
flags at similar rates (e.g.\ gpt-4o-mini \rfolRightMini\% \rfolRightMiniCI\ and \rfolWrongMini\% \rfolWrongMiniCI),
except gpt-6.1-terra (\rfolRightTerra\% and \rfolWrongTerra\%, one seed), so the break-even rises mainly because an
accurate generator has more correct answers to lose.

\paragraph{The randomised test predicts real detectors.} H24 crossed six detectors with six generators in
\hTwentyFourCells\ cells. For each cell we predict the flag's effect from the cell's own detections and no-flag
answers and the follow rates measured in H31's separate runs: a flag naming the false side on a question the
generator gets wrong gains its right-follow rate, and one marking a correct passage on a question it gets right costs
its wrong-follow rate. For the \predN\ cells whose generator had an H31 run, prediction and observation agree closely
($r=\predR$ \predRCI, slope \predSlope, intercept \predIntercept, mean error \predMae\ points; within generators
$r=\predRWithin$), and the prediction calls the harm the gpt-4o-mini detector does to gpt-6.1-terra. For the
\predTrN\ cells of generators without one, with the mean follow rates, $r=\predTrR$ but the prediction overshoots
(slope \predTrSlope; Figure~\ref{fig:reliability}, right). This prediction was defined after the cells were seen. The
registered H24 correlation ($\rho=\hTwentyFourRho$, $p\hTwentyFourP$) is uninformative: the generator's accuracy
enters both sides, and leaving out one detector it predicts held-out cells worse than their mean
($R^2=\hRedoCvGap$). Within
generators, direction precision and recall both matter ($p=\recPPerm$ and $\recRPerm$, exact permutation over the six
detectors; exploratory).
The gpt-4o-mini detector, the least precise (\dirPrecMini\% against \dirPrecOtherMin--\dirPrecOtherMax\%), helps the
weaker generators and lowers the accuracy of the four most accurate, significantly for gpt-6.1-terra (H19).

\paragraph{What the detector contributes is its verdict.} Passing the detector's judgement as an answer key (``the
answer $X$ is false'' for the answer its suspect passage supports, no passage marked) matches the directional flag
(H30: \hThirtyAMin\ to \hThirtyAMax\ points per cell, none significant; pooled over the \PoolThirtyAK\ cells the flag
is ahead by \PoolThirtyA\ \PoolThirtyACI, equivalent within $\pm$3) and beats the detector model's own answer as a
hint by \PoolThirtyB\ \PoolThirtyBCI. That hint does worse than the flag in \nHTwentySevenAPos\ of
\nHTwentySevenATests\ cells and never better (H27).

\paragraph{When conflicts are rare.} The LLM detector also flags clean questions (\cleanFprLlm\% of RAMDocs'), where
a directional flag can only hurt; at a 5\% share of conflict questions its flag lowers accuracy in \prevNegFive\ of
\prevN\ settings (Table~\ref{tab:prevalence}; exploratory).

\paragraph{Artefacts, genuine conflicts and baselines.} Reversing the passage order \citep{liu2024lost} or paraphrasing every passage changes the
detectors' hit rate by \hTwentyNineLunaRev\ to \hTwentyNineMiniPara\ points (H29), none significant, with equivalence
within $\pm$5 shown for one of four. Where both sides are valid, direction does damage (H28): on WikiContradict
the LLM directional flag lowers the share of answers giving both sides by \ahTwentyEightWcMiniDir\ points for
gpt-4o-mini, \ahTwentyEightWcLunaDir\ for gpt-6-luna and \ahTwentyEightWcHaikuDir\ for Claude Haiku, and on the
\hTwentyEightAmbMiniN\ RAMDocs same-name questions removal costs every generator \ahTwentyEightAmbMiniRem\ to
\ahTwentyEightAmbLunaRem\ points. In the registered ConflictBank comparison with Astute RAG and TrustRAG
our pipeline does not win (H14; Appendix~\ref{app:extra}).
