\subsection{A neutral flag trades misinformation for abstention}
\label{sec:neutral}
\input{generated/tab_risk}

Flagging exactly the gold contradictions in the neutral format lowers gpt-4o-mini's RAMDocs conflict-question accuracy
by \adOracleFlagConf\ points \dOracleFlagConfCI\ from \plainConf\% (H2), and gpt-4.1's by \agptOracleFlagConf\ (H9).
On ConflictBank, with a prompt stating that the question has one answer, it costs gpt-4o-mini
\azSgMiniOracleFlagConflict\ points, gpt-4.1 \azSgFourOracleFlagConflict\ and gpt-6-luna \azSgLunaOracleFlagConflict\
(H15, H19), but it does not significantly change accuracy for gpt-6.1-terra, Qwen2.5-7B, either Claude model (H25) or
any generator on RAGuard (H26). Pooled over the \nNeutralSettings\ settings in which we ran it
(Table~\ref{tab:pooled}; registered as exploratory), the effect is \neuPooled\ points, but settings differ more than
sampling explains: a new setting's effect would fall in \neuPI. A meta-regression places the harm in OpenAI models on
generated data with ``unknown'' allowed (\neuModBase\ points); Claude (\neuModAnthropic), a prompt without
``unknown'' (\neuModForced) and natural data (\neuModNatural) each shift the effect up.

\paragraph{The flag changes whether the model answers, not which side it picks.} With the one-answer prompt, of
gpt-4o-mini's answers that contained the misinformation without a flag, \trSgMiniNeutralMisinfoAbstain\% abstain with
the neutral flag and only \trSgMiniNeutralMisinfoCorrect\% become correct; \trSgMiniNeutralCorrectAbstain\% of its
correct answers also turn into abstentions (Table~\ref{tab:utility}). Accuracy on the answers it still gives moves by
\selNeuMin\ to \selNeuMax\ points across the eight ConflictBank settings of H21, significantly in \nSelNeuSig\ (Claude
Haiku, forced prompt); equivalence within $\pm$3 points is established in \nSelNeuEq. When ``unknown'' is not allowed
(H20), the neutral flag has no significant effect for gpt-4o-mini (\hTwentyFcMiniFlag), gpt-6-luna
(\hTwentyFcLunaFlag) or gpt-6.1-terra (\hTwentyFcTerraFlag), though equivalence is not established, and a small one
for Claude Haiku (\hTwentyFcHaikuFlag). Under the multi-answer prompt it makes the model list both
sides (Figure~\ref{fig:answers}); on WikiContradict, where both are valid, that raises accuracy (H8: \wikiOracleFlag).

\paragraph{Whether that trade helps depends on what a wrong answer costs.} Strict accuracy counts an abstention and a
misinformed answer alike. If a misinformed answer instead costs $c$ correct ones (abstaining costs nothing), the
neutral flag significantly lowers utility in \nUtilNeuNegZero\ of \nUtilSettings\ settings at $c=0$ and raises it in
\nUtilNeuPosZero; at $c=4$ it raises it in \nUtilNeuPosFour\ and lowers it in \nUtilNeuNegFour\
(Table~\ref{tab:utility}); for gpt-4o-mini on ConflictBank it moves from \utSgMiniNeutralZero\ to
\utSgMiniNeutralFour.

\subsection{Naming the false side: what an oracle can deliver}
\label{sec:direction}
With the same oracle pairs, the directional flag beats the neutral and one-wrong flags in every registered comparison
(H3, H9--H12, H15, H16, H19, H25, H26). On ConflictBank it gains between \azSgTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ and
\azSgLunaOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ points for the OpenAI generators and \azSgSonnetOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\
to \azSgHaikuOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ for Claude; it gains under the forced prompt (H20: \hTwentyFcTerraDir\ to
\hTwentyFcLunaDir), when the misinformation outnumbers the truth three to one (\zMjMiniOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\
to \zMjTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict), and on RAGuard's real posts (H26: \hTwentySixRgTerraDir\ to
\hTwentySixRgSonnetDir). On RAMDocs it gains \adOracleDirConf\ points with gpt-4o-mini and up to
\azRdTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ with gpt-6.1-terra, but not significantly for Qwen2.5-7B
(Table~\ref{tab:generality}).

These rows are a ceiling, not a detector result. Telling the model ``the answer $X$ is false'', with no passage
marked, recovers most of the gain: no registered comparison differs significantly (H23), the directional flag's
advantage ranges from \keyDiffMin\ to \keyDiffMax\ points, and within $\pm$3 points equivalence holds in \nTostTwentyThreeEq\ of
\nTostTwentyThreeTests\ (TOST); for gpt-4o-mini on ConflictBank the flag is ahead (90\% CI \tostKeySgMini). An inverted oracle that names the correct side costs
\aadvDirConf\ points on RAMDocs.

Saying only that one side is false helps when the model must answer. Where it may abstain, the one-wrong flag lowers
gpt-4o-mini's ConflictBank accuracy by \azSgMiniOracleFlagOnewrongConflict\ points (H16); under the forced prompt it
raises every generator's (H20: \hTwentyFcHaikuOw\ to \hTwentyFcLunaOw), and on RAGuard it helps Claude Haiku
(\hTwentySixRgHaikuOw) but hurts gpt-6-luna (\hTwentySixRgLunaOw). Removing the pair structure has no consistent effect (H22).

\subsection{With a real detector, where its errors fall decides}
\label{sec:real}
\paragraph{Registered results.} With the gpt-4o-mini LLM detector, the directional flag significantly raises
accuracy over all ConflictBank questions for \nRealSgPos\ of \nRealSgTests\ generators (gpt-6-luna,
\zSgLunaLlmDirFlagDirectionalLabelled) and significantly lowers it for \nRealSgNeg\ (gpt-6.1-terra,
\zSgTerraLlmDirFlagDirectionalLabelled; H15, H19, H25). It helps all \nRealMjTests\ generators tested under majority
misinformation (\zMjMiniLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\ to \zMjTerraLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict), and on RAGuard it
helps the two Claude models (H26: \hTwentySixRgHaikuLlm, \hTwentySixRgSonnetLlm) but none of the OpenAI ones.

\paragraph{The registered reliability test does not isolate the detector.}

H24 crossed six detectors with six generators in \hTwentyFourCells\ cells. As registered, the gain rises with the
detector's direction accuracy minus the generator's no-flag accuracy ($\rho=\hTwentyFourRho$, $p\hTwentyFourP$),
including on the \hRedoPostCells\ cells that did not exist when H24 was registered ($\rho=\hRedoPostRho$). But the
generator's accuracy $G$ enters both the predictor and the gain, and most of the correlation comes from it. Regressing
the flagged accuracy on direction accuracy $D$ and $G$ gives $D$ a coefficient of \hRedoAddDCoef\ against
\hRedoAddGCoef\ for $G$; with generator fixed effects, permuting $D$ across the six detectors gives $p=\hRedoPermP$
(\hRedoNPerm\ permutations). Leaving out one detector at a time, the registered gap predicts the held-out cells' gains
worse than their mean ($R^2=\hRedoCvGap$), and worse than $G$ alone (\hRedoCvG; Table~\ref{tab:h24redo}). A detector's
overall direction accuracy says little about whether it will help.

\paragraph{Where its errors fall does.} Per question, with detector, generator and question effects, a flag that names
the false side raises the chance of a correct answer by \ahRedoMixD\ points \hRedoMixDCI, and one that marks a correct
passage lowers it by \ahRedoMixW\ \hRedoMixWCI. Generators follow a right flag they need on \cmpRightMin--\cmpRightMax\%
of questions and a wrong-side flag on \cmpWrongMin--\cmpWrongMax\%: they act on a wrong flag more readily than on a
right one. A cell's gain is therefore set by how often the detector is right where the generator is wrong and wrong
where the generator is right. The exploratory score built from these two counts predicts held-out detectors' cells
($R^2=\hRedoCvMech$; calibration slope \hRedoCalSlope\ \hRedoCalSlopeCI; Figure~\ref{fig:reliability}, left). The
gpt-4o-mini detector flags almost every question (\detAnyFlagMini\%) and marks a correct passage on
\detWrongDirMini\% of them: it helps the weaker generators and lowers the accuracy of the four most accurate, significantly only for
gpt-6.1-terra (H19). gpt-6.1-terra flags \detAnyFlagTerra\% and marks a correct passage on
\detWrongDirTerra\%; it lowers no generator's accuracy.

\paragraph{It is not just a stronger model's answer (H27).} If a cell's gain were the detector model's better answer
passed along, giving the generator that answer as a hint would do as well. It does not: the directional flag beats
the hint significantly in \nHTwentySevenAPos\ of \nHTwentySevenATests\ cells and loses to it in \nHTwentySevenANeg\
(Figure~\ref{fig:reliability}, right). The hint itself helps in \nHTwentySevenBPos\ cells and hurts in
\nHTwentySevenBNeg, mostly the more accurate generators, which lose accuracy even when the hinted answer is better
than their own. Using the detector model as the generator instead does significantly worse than the cell in
\nHTwentySevenCPos\ cells and better in \nHTwentySevenCNeg, each a much stronger detector model (gpt-6.1-terra or
Claude Sonnet) serving a weaker generator.

\paragraph{Not a surface artefact (H29).} ConflictBank's two sides are written by an LLM from different prompts, so
a detector could key on style or position. Reversing the passage order or paraphrasing every passage with one model
leaves gpt-4o-mini's direction accuracy at \hTwentyNineMiniRevProbe\% and \hTwentyNineMiniParaProbe\% (from
\hTwentyNineMiniRevOrig\%) and gpt-6-luna's at \hTwentyNineLunaRevProbe\% and \hTwentyNineLunaParaProbe\% (from
\hTwentyNineLunaRevOrig\%); no change is significant, and neither is the change in the generator's gain (H29c).

\paragraph{Genuine conflicts (H28).} Where both sides are valid, direction does damage. On WikiContradict the LLM
directional flag lowers the share of answers giving both sides by \ahTwentyEightWcMiniDir\ points for gpt-4o-mini,
\ahTwentyEightWcLunaDir\ for gpt-6-luna and \ahTwentyEightWcHaikuDir\ for Claude Haiku (from \hTwentyEightWcMiniPlain,
\hTwentyEightWcLunaPlain\ and \hTwentyEightWcHaikuPlain\%), and removal by more; the oracle neutral flag raises it. On
the \hTwentyEightAmbMiniN\ RAMDocs questions whose conflicts are same-name ambiguity, removal costs every generator
\ahTwentyEightAmbMiniRem\ to \ahTwentyEightAmbLunaRem\ points and the directional flag up to
\ahTwentyEightAmbLunaDir\ (gpt-6-luna). The detector calls a valid alternative misinformation, and the generator drops it.

\paragraph{Baselines.} Astute RAG \citep{wang2025astute} and TrustRAG \citep{zhou2025trustrag} lose legitimate
same-name answers on RAMDocs (H14), and a single call that names suspect passages before answering does not differ
significantly from the detector pipeline (H18; Table~\ref{tab:baselines}). The synthetic grid (H4, H6) is in
Appendix~\ref{app:grid}.
