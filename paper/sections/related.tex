\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Knowledge conflicts.}
Language models can give priority to memorised knowledge over contradicting context \citep{longpre2021entity}.
They are also readily persuaded by coherent external evidence that contradicts their memory \citep{xie2024chameleon},
and they adopt wrong context over a correct prior to a degree that depends on how strong that prior is
\citep{wu2024clasheval}. \citet{xu2024survey} distinguish context--memory, inter-context and intra-memory conflicts.
We study inter-context conflicts: disagreements among retrieved passages, as in \citet{chen2022rich}, who find that
models tend to rely on a single passage when the evidence conflicts.

\paragraph{Benchmarks with conflicting evidence.}
RAMDocs \citep{wang2025madam} mixes genuine ambiguity (several valid answers), misinformation and noise across
retrieved documents, and builds on AmbigDocs \citep{lee2024ambigdocs}. CONFLICTS \citep{cattan2025conflicts}
annotates search results with a taxonomy of conflict types, each with its own desired behaviour.
WikiContradict \citep{hou2024wikicontradict} pairs Wikipedia passages that genuinely contradict each other.
FaithEval \citep{ming2025faitheval}, ConflictBank \citep{su2024conflictbank} and ConfRAG \citep{yuan2026confrag}
cover related settings. We use RAMDocs because its document labels say which side of a contradiction is the
misinformation, and WikiContradict as a contrast case where neither side is.

\paragraph{Telling the model about conflicts.}
The closest prior work tells the generator that a conflict exists. \citet{cattan2025conflicts} find that prompting a model to
reason about the conflict type helps, and that supplying the gold conflict type helps much more. On WikiContradict,
models acknowledge both sides more often when told the passages may conflict \citep{hou2024wikicontradict}.
Ragability \citep{gross2026ragability} shows the cost of such a hint: it improves conflict identification on
contradictory contexts but hurts it on consistent ones. We extend these findings from conflict identification to
answer accuracy. We separate the \emph{content} of the hint (neutral, directional, removal) from its \emph{accuracy}
(precision, recall, direction), and we show that a neutral hint can hurt even when it is perfectly accurate.

\paragraph{Conflict detection and resolution in RAG.}
ConflictRAG \citep{wang2026conflictrag} trains a pairwise detector on MiniLM embeddings with an LLM fallback, and
reports pair-level detection F1 alongside answer gains. Its baselines include an NLI filter and a single-call
chain-of-thought detector, both of which we reimplement in spirit. \citet{gokul2025contradiction} find that LLMs
are imperfect context validators whose accuracy varies with contradiction type. TCR \citep{ye2026tcr} passes conflict
signals to the generator through a learned soft prompt, and EvidentialRAG \citep{hossain2026evidentialrag} routes
between answering and abstaining on fused evidential uncertainty. Credibility-aware generation
\citep{pan2024credibility} fine-tunes models to weigh per-passage credibility scores, the trained analogue of our
directional flags. MADAM-RAG \citep{wang2025madam} resolves conflicts implicitly, through debate among
per-document agents. Astute RAG \citep{wang2025astute}, Self-RAG \citep{asai2024selfrag} and Corrective RAG
\citep{yan2024crag} assess passage reliability without an explicit contradiction detector. Our question is
orthogonal to all of these: given a detector's output, which way of passing it to the generator helps, and what must
the detector get right for that to happen?

\paragraph{Poisoned retrieval.}
A few injected passages can steer a RAG system's answer \citep{zou2025poisonedrag}. Isolating passages and
aggregating their answers gives certifiable robustness \citep{xiang2024robustrag}. Our removal results connect to
this line of work: removing the suspected passage is the strongest intervention we test, and also the one most
damaged by false flags, which an attacker could trigger.
