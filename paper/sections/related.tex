\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Knowledge conflicts.}
Language models can prefer memorised knowledge over contradicting context \citep{longpre2021entity}, yet are readily
persuaded by coherent contradicting evidence \citep{xie2024chameleon}, to a degree that depends on the strength of
their prior \citep{wu2024clasheval}. Of the conflict types of \citet{xu2024survey}, we study inter-context
conflicts, as in \citet{chen2022rich}, who find that models tend to rely on a single passage when evidence conflicts.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
RAMDocs \citep{wang2025madam}, built on AmbigDocs \citep{lee2024ambigdocs}, mixes genuine ambiguity, misinformation
and noise. CONFLICTS \citep{cattan2025conflicts} annotates search results with conflict types, each with its own
desired behaviour, and WikiContradict \citep{hou2024wikicontradict} pairs genuinely contradicting Wikipedia passages.
FaithEval \citep{ming2025faitheval}, ConflictBank \citep{su2024conflictbank} and ConfRAG \citep{yuan2026confrag}
cover related settings. RAMDocs and ConflictBank label which side is misinformation; WikiContradict does not.

\paragraph{Telling the model about conflicts.}
Prompting a model to reason about the conflict type helps, and supplying the gold type helps much more
\citep{cattan2025conflicts}; on WikiContradict, models acknowledge both sides more often when told the passages may
conflict \citep{hou2024wikicontradict}. Ragability \citep{gross2026ragability} finds that such a hint helps on
contradictory contexts but hurts on \emph{consistent} ones, where it is false. In our neutral-flag result the hint is
true and the input contradictory, and accuracy still falls, because the hint does not say which side to trust.
\citet{pan2023risk} test detection and ``vigilant'' prompting against LLM-generated misinformation, and
\citet{pan2023attacking} call for misinformation-aware QA. Models weigh retrieved pages' metadata and appearance
\citep{chiang2024metadata}, and source-credibility signals help a fact-checker resolve conflicting evidence
\citep{ge2025confact}. We separate the \emph{content} of the hint from its \emph{accuracy}.

\paragraph{Telling the model which passage is wrong.}
Closest to our directional format, \citet{hong2024gullible} pass a discriminator's marks on perturbed passages to the
generator in the prompt, which makes it more robust to counterfactual noise. CrAM \citep{deng2025cram} rescales
attention by passage credibility, RA-RAG \citep{hwang2025rarag} estimates source reliability, and
\citet{weller2024defending} defend open-domain QA against disinformation. These works show that \emph{some}
reliability signal helps, but do not compare it with a neutral warning on the same pairs or measure its cost on
conflict-free questions. RGB \citep{chen2024rgb} warns models that passages may contain errors, and they still rarely
detect them; our neutral flag is the pair-specific version of that warning.

\paragraph{Detection, resolution and poisoning.}
ConflictRAG \citep{wang2026conflictrag} trains a pairwise detector with an LLM fallback and reports pair-level F1
alongside answer gains; its NLI and chain-of-thought baselines resemble two of our detectors. LLMs are imperfect
context validators \citep{gokul2025contradiction}, detect conflicts but struggle to pinpoint them
\citep{wang2024resolving}, and tend to pick a side without explanation \citep{jiayang2024econ}. They favour the more
frequent side and the one matching their prior \citep{jin2024tugofwar,kortukov2024realistic}, prefer LLM-generated
passages \citep{tan2024blinded} (both ConflictBank sides are generated), are distracted by irrelevant context
\citep{shi2023distracted}, and favour some sources \citep{schuster2026whose}. TCR \citep{ye2026tcr} uses a learned
soft prompt, context-aware decoding \citep{shi2024cad} upweights the context, EvidentialRAG
\citep{hossain2026evidentialrag} routes between answering and abstaining, and credibility-aware generation
\citep{pan2024credibility} fine-tunes models to weigh passage credibility, a trained analogue of our directional
flags. MADAM-RAG \citep{wang2025madam}, Astute RAG \citep{wang2025astute}, Self-RAG \citep{asai2024selfrag},
Corrective RAG \citep{yan2024crag} and InstructRAG \citep{wei2025instructrag} handle unreliable passages without an
explicit contradiction detector; we compare against Astute RAG and TrustRAG. A few injected passages can steer a RAG
system \citep{zou2025poisonedrag}; RobustRAG \citep{xiang2024robustrag} isolates passages for certifiable robustness,
and TrustRAG \citep{zhou2025trustrag} filters suspected poisoned ones, the analogue of our removal. We ask instead how
a detector's output should be passed to the generator.
