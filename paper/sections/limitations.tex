\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Models.} We test two closed OpenAI generators. Open-weights models may respond differently to flags,
particularly to the directional header, and we could not run one within this study. The LLM detector is a single
prompt to one model; stronger or fine-tuned detectors would occupy different points on the map.

\paragraph{Data.} The confirmatory evidence is one benchmark, RAMDocs, with 300 questions. Its misinformation is
synthetic and its document labels are imperfect: we exclude 55 questions whose status depends on 163 documents we
could not label without human annotation, and we check that conclusions survive treating those documents as
misinformation. WikiContradict and CONFLICTS each test one side of our account (genuine conflicts and real search
results), but neither has a misinformation side that we can label at the passage level. We score CONFLICTS only on
items with a reference answer and use no LLM judge, so its opinion and complementary-information items are not
evaluated.

\paragraph{Metric.} RAMDocs ships no scorer. Our primary metric reconstructs the published definition, and with it
MADAM-RAG reproduces the published score within about one point on our pilot split. It is still string matching:
it misses paraphrases, and partial answers interact with ``no wrong answer''. We report a more lenient variant
throughout and mark any conclusion that depends on the choice.

\paragraph{Synthetic detectors.} Synthetic false flags are spread uniformly over non-contradicting pairs, and their
suspect side is chosen at random. Real detectors concentrate their errors, and the grid predicts them only once it
is summarised at the document level. We chose those axes after seeing one detector on the pilot split; on the
confirmatory split they are tested on three detectors, which is still a small sample.

\paragraph{Scope.} We study flags passed to the generator as text. Learned interfaces such as soft prompts or
credibility fine-tuning \citep{ye2026tcr,pan2024credibility}, and retrieval-side filtering, may follow different
trade-offs.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work uses public benchmarks and commercial model APIs, and involves no human subjects or personal data. Its main
risk is dual use. Our results show that a false ``likely misinformation'' label, or the removal of a passage, can
change a RAG system's answers. An adversary who can trigger a deployed detector could exploit this
\citep{zou2025poisonedrag}. We therefore report harm on conflict-free questions alongside every gain, and we argue that
systems should measure what a detector does at the document level before acting on its flags.
