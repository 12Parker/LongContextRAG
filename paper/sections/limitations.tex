\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{ConflictBank's false side is readable from style.} The randomised-direction test, the follow rates,
$p^*$, the crossed cells and the prospective test all use ConflictBank, whose two on-topic passages are both
LLM-written around a substituted object. A bag-of-words classifier trained on other ConflictBank facts picks the
misinformation passage on every conflict question of our eval set and of ConflictBank-fresh, also with the answer
strings masked, and its
heaviest words are genre words (R1, Appendix~\ref{app:rev11}). Detectors also name the false side more often on facts
their own model knows closed-book (R2), and the generators know few of these facts (closed-book accuracy
\cbClosedMin--\cbClosedMax\% for the \cbClosedN\ generators run closed-book). A detector's direction and a generator's no-flag accuracy can therefore come from
style rather than from knowledge or from reasoning about the contradiction. H31 assigns direction by coin flip, so its
follow rates still measure deference to the flag; but the no-flag accuracies, the real detectors' direction precision,
$p^*$ and the account were all measured where the truth is legible from style. Whether they hold for real
misinformation is untested, and we did not run the single-passage and answer-only probes that would separate a style
cue from reasoning about the contradiction; R1 shows only that the cue exists. ConflictBank's clean questions add only off-subject passages, so they test off-topic false
flags only.

\paragraph{Scorer and human audit.} Every claim in the body is scored by string matching. The matcher's misses are one-sided and
depend on the condition: it misses more correct answers under directional flags than under answer keys
(Appendix~\ref{app:fresh}). Under an outside-family LLM judge neither pooled key equivalence holds (H38), so H23 and
H30 are unresolved. The follow rates, $p^*$ and the prospective test have no full judge re-score, and
gpt-6.1-terra's follow rates have none at all. No human has labelled any output: the blind disagreement sheet that
could settle the scorer question and the paired audit sheets are released unlabelled (Appendix~\ref{app:semantic}).
On a \semNq-question sample the outside-family judge makes Claude Haiku's ConflictBank neutral-flag effect
\semNeuCBsHaikuJ\ (matcher: \semNeuCBsHaikuM). Responses with no final-answer line are scored wrong, as registered;
they exceed 1\% in \missCellsAbove\ of \missCells\ run--condition cells (R9). In the body these are the four settings
named under Figure~\ref{fig:riskcov} and gpt-6-luna's closed-book cell in Table~\ref{tab:deference}; \prs{20.3}
asked main-text tables to mark such cells, and Table~\ref{tab:deference} does not.

\paragraph{Real documents and other benchmarks.} HealthContradict's labels are as released, and an LLM judge confirms
both pages of an item for only \stanceBoth\% of items (R13). Label noise could therefore explain why the account
overstated harm there, and with one page per side its oracle flag works close to an answer key. RAGuard's labels are
behavioural: a judge finds only \rgAudContradict\% of sampled supporting--misleading pairs to contradict, and all four
generators run closed-book are more accurate without the posts. We treat both as label-transfer stress tests.
CONFLICTS has few misinformation conflicts and no passage labels, so it can neither confirm nor refute our findings.
RAMDocs' document labels are imperfect and its pair rule does not align entities (\S\ref{sec:setup}). Our
conflict-question comparisons detect effects of about \mdeMed\ points with 80\% power; smaller effects, and small
harms on clean questions, may be missed.

\paragraph{Registration.} H1--H9 were registered before any confirmatory data. H10--H31 and H37--H38 were registered
in addenda committed before their runs but after earlier results were known; three items were committed after runs
had started (\prs{20.1}). H32--H36 froze numeric predictions before their outcomes existed. Commit times are local;
push times to the public remote are the only third-party record (Table~\ref{tab:timeline}), and we used no registry.
Each addendum followed a review of earlier results, so registration fixes how an analysis is run more than which
analyses are chosen. The $\pm$3 TOST margin was set after the confirmatory run, and the pooled TOSTs were added after
the per-comparison ones. The account's form was chosen after the crossed cells were seen; only its frozen predictions (H32--H36) are
confirmatory. $p^*$, prevalence and every \prs{20} re-analysis are exploratory. H24 is mis-specified: generator accuracy enters both sides of its tests, so its support is mechanical.
Holm controls error within each family, not across the programme: of \fdrN\ registered tests, \fdrHolm\ are
significant under Holm and \fdrBH\ under a Benjamini--Hochberg FDR over all tests.

\paragraph{Models and runs.} We recorded the model names we called between 2026-10-26 and 2026-10-29, not the served
snapshots. gpt-6.1-terra, Claude Sonnet, Qwen2.5-7B, and gpt-4.1 outside RAMDocs ran one seed. gpt-6.1-terra's follow
rates and $p^*$ rest on one default-temperature draw; its other no-flag draw moves $p^*$ from \terraDrawRegBe\
to \terraDrawAltBe\ (R7). The reasoning and Claude models ran only at default temperature, Claude through an
OpenAI-compatible endpoint without JSON mode. We draw no conclusion about model families: two Claude models and one
4-bit 7B open model cannot support one, and family is confounded with model age and capability.

\paragraph{Detectors and scope.} Detector claims cover the detectors we tested: one LLM-detector prompt run on
several models, CAAG, an NLI cross-encoder and synthetic detectors. We test no trained detector such as ConflictRAG's
\citep{wang2026conflictrag}. Under its registered window NLI flags no ConflictBank pair; the 300-character window that
does was chosen after seeing detection output (\prs{17.6}). The published baselines are reimplementations (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}). We study
flags passed to the generator as text; learned interfaces such as soft prompts or credibility fine-tuning
\citep{ye2026tcr,pan2024credibility}, and retrieval-side filtering, may trade off differently. Flags that state the detector's confidence are untested (only CAAG's
original format, Appendix~\ref{app:extra}), so whether stated confidence changes $f_R$ and $f_W$ is open. We test one
adversarial case, a detector that always names the wrong side, not an attacker who chooses the flagged pairs.

\paragraph{What would test these claims.} Claims 2 and 3 need a benchmark whose false side is not legible from
style and whose facts the generators know; ConflictBank offers neither, so we do not know how far the follow rates
carry beyond it. The answer-key question needs human labels on the outputs where the two scorers disagree. The
account has met only \acctHarmN\ predicted harms; more cells where a real detector is expected to hurt would show
whether it predicts the case that matters most. The decision rule (\S\ref{sec:discussion}), derived from these runs, has not
been tested end to end; that needs a benchmark with on-topic clean questions at a realistic conflict share.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work uses public benchmarks and commercial model APIs, with no human subjects. RAGuard's passages are
public Reddit posts that can contain usernames; we use them as released, and our released RAGuard audit sheet replaces private users' handles with placeholders. The main
risk is dual use: a false ``likely misinformation'' label, or removing a passage, can
change a RAG system's answers. An inverted oracle that names the correct side as misinformation costs \aadvDirConf\
points on RAMDocs and \azCbMiniOracleInvFlagDirectionalConflict\ on ConflictBank (multi-answer prompt) as a flag, and \aadvRemConf\ on
RAMDocs when the passage it names is removed. An adversary who can trigger a deployed detector could exploit this
\citep{zou2025poisonedrag}. We therefore report harm on clean questions alongside every gain, and argue that
systems should measure a detector's document-level behaviour before acting on its flags.
