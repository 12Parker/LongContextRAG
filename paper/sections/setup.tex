\paragraph{Data.}
Table~\ref{tab:evidence} gives each benchmark's role. \textbf{RAMDocs} \citep{wang2025madam} gives each question 1--12 documents typed correct, misinformation or noise. A
pair is a \emph{contradiction} if one side is misinformation and the answers differ. A \emph{conflict} question has a
contradiction pair; a \emph{clean} question has none. On the 300-question hold-out split (139 conflict, 126 clean, 35 excluded),
\cleanTypeNSeveral\ clean questions have several valid answers, and the pairing rule does not align same-name
entities, so at least \pairCrossLbShare\% of oracle pairs join passages about different entities
(Appendix~\ref{app:semantic}).

\textbf{ConflictBank} \citep{su2024conflictbank}: for a Wikidata fact, an LLM writes one passage for the true object
and one for a substituted object. We sample 300 conflict questions (correct passage, misinformation passage, two
distractors about other subjects) and 100 clean ones (correct passage, three distractors); each has one gold answer.
A \emph{majority} variant gives each conflict question three misinformation passages. ConflictBank-fresh, 400 more
questions with no shared subject, serves the prospective test. Three properties limit what ConflictBank can show.
The generators rarely know the facts: closed-book accuracy is \cbClosedMin--\cbClosedMax\% for every
generator run without passages. With one passage per side, agreement between passages cannot reveal the false side.
And the false side is legible from style. A bag-of-words logistic regression, trained on \clfTrainRows\ ConflictBank
facts that share no subject with ours, gives the misinformation passage the higher score in every conflict pair of
both samples (pairwise accuracy \clfFullEval), also with each passage's answer string masked (\clfMaskEval). Its
heaviest features are genre words, such as \clfTopFalse\ for the false side (R1, exploratory). Its clean questions test only false flags on off-subject passages.

\textbf{Stress tests.} RAGuard \citep{zeng2025raguard} pairs PolitiFact claims with Reddit posts that an LLM
labelled misleading or supporting according to whether GPT-4 judged the claim correctly with the post in context;
an LLM judge finds only \rgAudContradict\% of sampled pairs to contradict.
HealthContradict \citep{zhang2026healthcontradict} pairs yes/no health questions with one real web page per side
(247 items, pages cut to 4,000 characters). Neither verifies that the labelled-misleading side is false; on
HealthContradict an LLM judge confirms both pages' labels for only \hcLabBoth\% of items. We therefore treat both as
label-transfer stress tests. \textbf{WikiContradict} \citep{hou2024wikicontradict} pairs genuinely contradicting
Wikipedia passages; a correct answer names both sides. CONFLICTS \citep{cattan2025conflicts} (237 questions) has too
few misinformation conflicts to detect effects. Appendix~\ref{app:repro} details every benchmark.

\paragraph{Generators.}
The main generator is gpt-4o-mini \citep{openai2024gpt4o} (temperature 0.1, at most 256 output tokens, seeds 0--2).
We also run gpt-4.1, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct \citep{yang2024qwen25} (4-bit, local), two reasoning models (gpt-6-luna,
three seeds; gpt-6.1-terra, one seed), Claude Haiku 5.5 (three seeds) and Claude Sonnet 5.5 (one seed). The
reasoning and Claude models run at their default temperature with up to 4,096 output tokens; Claude runs without
JSON mode (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). On ConflictBank the main prompt is the one-answer skeleton.

\paragraph{Scorers.}
The primary scorer, fixed before the confirmatory run, is a one-way string matcher (bidirectional for WikiContradict's
both-sides score): an answer is correct if, after
normalisation, every gold answer and no wrong answer is a whole-word span of its final-answer line
\citep{wang2025madam}; the \emph{bidirectional} variant, for robustness, also accepts an answer inside the gold
string. \emph{Misinformation share} is the share of answers containing a wrong answer;
\emph{selective accuracy} is accuracy among answers that do not abstain. An outside-family LLM judge
(gemini-3.5-flash-lite) re-scored all \jdN\ responses of the pooled key comparisons and agrees with the matcher on
\jdAgree\%. The matcher's misses are one-sided and depend on the condition: the judge alone calls correct
\scJudgeOnlyOracleDir\% of oracle directional-flag responses, against \scJudgeOnlyAnswerKey\% of answer-key
responses (Appendix~\ref{app:semantic}). The
matcher thus misses more correct answers under the flags than under the keys, so a contrast near zero must hold under
both scorers. Responses without a final-answer line are scored on their whole text, as registered (\prs{5}), so some count as
correct; they are \missShare\% of all responses, but
\missCellsAbove\ of \missCells\ run--condition cells exceed 1\%, up to \missTopShare\% in one \missTopModel\ condition
(R9, Table~\ref{tab:missing}). No human has yet labelled outputs; blind audit sheets are released unlabelled, and they and a within-family judge
check are described in Appendix~\ref{app:semantic}.

\paragraph{Registration.}
``\prs{N}'' is section N of our pre-registration. We registered in three tiers. (i) H1--H9 were frozen
before any confirmatory data, after pilots on 200 RAMDocs questions. Thirteen addenda followed (\prs{9}--\prs{21}); ten
carry hypotheses, and \prs{20} registers only re-analyses (R1--R19). (ii) H10--H31, H37--H38 and H40--H41 were registered before their runs but after earlier results were
known; three items were committed after their runs had begun (\prs{20.1}), and parts of H24, H27 and H28 reuse runs that
predate them (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). (iii) H32--H36 are prospective: their
predictions were committed before any flagged answer existed. We call only tiers (i) and (iii) confirmatory (commit times: Table~\ref{tab:timeline}); tier (ii) is registered but
not confirmatory. Everything else is exploratory, including R1--R19 and the descriptive \prs{21} runs C1--C11
(Appendices~\ref{app:rev11}, \ref{app:rev21}).

\paragraph{Statistics.}
We average each question's score over seeds and report paired differences with question-bootstrap 95\% CIs and
sign-flip permutation tests \citep{efron1993bootstrap,dror2018hitchhiker}. A registered test takes the Holm-adjusted
$p$ \citep{holm1979simple} within its hypothesis's family; the exploratory condition comparisons form one Holm family
per metric (\nExploratory\ for accuracy). ``Significant'' means under Holm; CIs are unadjusted. A null difference is not read as equivalence
without a TOST \citep{lakens2017equivalence} at $\pm$3 points, a margin set after the confirmatory run. By the APIs'
usage reports, all runs cost \totalSpend\ (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
