\section{Introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) \citep{lewis2020rag} hands a language model passages that may disagree. Some
disagreements are genuine: two sources report different findings, or two people share a name. Others are planted or
stale misinformation \citep{zou2025poisonedrag,wang2025madam}. A natural fix is to detect the contradiction and tell
the generator. Recent systems pair contradiction detectors with prompts that flag conflicting passages
\citep{wang2026conflictrag}, and prompting a model about conflicts can help \citep{cattan2025conflicts}. These systems
report detection F1 and end-to-end accuracy, but not how the two connect. What should the flag say, and what must the
detector get right for it to help?

We fix the retrieved passages and vary, independently, the \emph{detector} (heuristic, NLI, LLM, random placebo,
oracle, and synthetic detectors of chosen quality) and the \emph{format} that passes its output to the generator: a
neutral ``these passages may conflict'' flag, a flag that says one side is false without saying which, a
\emph{directional} flag that names the likely misinformation, or removal of the suspect passage. After pilots on
RAMDocs \citep{wang2025madam}, we pre-registered our hypotheses and tested them on 300 held-out questions; six
addenda, each committed before its runs, extended the study to ConflictBank \citep{su2024conflictbank} (including a
variant where the misinformation outnumbers the truth), to RAGuard \citep{zeng2025raguard}, whose misleading side is
real Reddit posts, and to seven generators from OpenAI, Anthropic and Qwen. Controls separate what a flag conveys: a
forced-choice prompt with no ``unknown'', flags stripped of their pair structure, an answer key that names the wrong
answer without pointing at any passage, and a crossed design in which six detectors serve every generator.
WikiContradict \citep{hou2024wikicontradict}, whose conflicts are genuine, is the contrast case.

Our findings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A correct neutral flag does not help against misinformation.} Pooled over \nNeutralSettings\ generator,
benchmark and prompt settings, flagging exactly the true contradictions changes accuracy by \neuPooled\ points
\neuPooledCI; it significantly lowers accuracy in \nNeutralHarm\ settings and raises it in \nNeutralHelp. It moves
answers into hedging or abstention but does not change which side the model picks: under a forced-choice prompt
its effect is null for three of four generators (H20), and accuracy on answered questions barely changes (H21).
The same flag \emph{raises} accuracy on WikiContradict, where both sides are valid.
\item \textbf{Naming the false side is what helps.} The directional flag raises accuracy on every benchmark, for
OpenAI, Anthropic and open models, including when the misinformation outnumbers the truth and on real Reddit posts.
It does about as well as telling the model the wrong answer outright (H23), so what it conveys is the
direction, not the location of the conflict. Saying that one side is false, without which, helps only when the
other passages reveal which.
\item \textbf{With a real detector, wrong-side flags are the cost.} Across \hTwentyFourCells\ crossed
detector--generator cells the directional flag's gain rises with the detector's reliability relative to the
generator's (pre-registered H24, $\rho=\hTwentyFourRho$, $p\hTwentyFourP$). An exploratory accounting makes this
precise: credit the detector for naming the false side where the generator would have been wrong, charge it for
pointing at the correct passage where the generator would have been right ($\rho=\hTwentyFourMechRho$). Silence is
cheap: the gpt-4o-mini detector marks a correct passage as suspect on \detWrongDirMini\% of questions and lowers the
accuracy of the four generators most accurate on their own, while reasoning-model detectors flag less, rarely err, and lower no generator's.
\end{itemize}
For conflict-aware RAG, we recommend passing direction rather than a bare warning, letting the detector abstain, and
measuring how often it names the wrong side on questions the generator already answers correctly. We release the
harness, pre-registrations and every run.\footnote{Code, the pre-registration and all run outputs are in the
anonymised supplementary material and will be released publicly on acceptance.}
