\section{Introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) \citep{lewis2020rag} hands a language model passages that may disagree. Some
disagreements are genuine: two sources report different findings, or two people share a name. Others are planted or
stale misinformation \citep{zou2025poisonedrag,wang2025madam}. A natural fix is to detect the contradiction and tell
the generator. Recent systems pair contradiction detectors with prompts that flag conflicting passages
\citep{wang2026conflictrag}, and prompting a model about conflicts can help \citep{cattan2025conflicts}. These systems
report detection F1 and end-to-end accuracy, but not how the two connect. What should the flag say, and what must the
detector get right for it to help?

We fix the retrieved passages and vary, independently, the \emph{detector} (a heuristic, an NLI cross-encoder, an
LLM, a random placebo and an oracle) and the \emph{format} that passes its output to the generator: a neutral ``these
passages may conflict'' flag, a \emph{directional} flag that names the likely misinformation, or removal of the
suspect passage. Synthetic detectors with a chosen precision, recall and direction accuracy show what detector quality
buys. After pilots on 200 RAMDocs questions \citep{wang2025madam}, we pre-registered our hypotheses and tested them on
300 held-out questions. Three addenda, each committed before its runs, added ConflictBank \citep{su2024conflictbank}
(which had no pilot) under three answer policies and with the misinformation in the majority, five generators
including two current reasoning models, two published conflict-robust methods, a closed-book control, and two
controls that separate \emph{what} a flag says: one that says one side is false without saying which, and one that
adds an instruction to drop unreliable passages. WikiContradict \citep{hou2024wikicontradict}, whose conflicts are
genuine, is the contrast case. Our claims concern injected misinformation scored by strict exact match; we do not
claim they carry over to open-ended answers.

Our findings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A correct neutral flag almost never helps against misinformation.} Flagging exactly the true
contradictions lowers gpt-4o-mini's accuracy on RAMDocs conflict questions by \adOracleFlagConf\ points
\dOracleFlagConfCI, and on ConflictBank by \azSgMiniOracleFlagConflict\ even when the prompt says the question has one
answer. Across five generators, two benchmarks and three answer policies, it significantly lowers accuracy in
\nNeutralHarm\ of \nNeutralSettings\ settings and raises it in \nNeutralHelp\ (gpt-6-luna under majority
misinformation; exploratory). The answer policy only decides
how it fails: the model adds the misinformation when asked for every valid answer, and abstains when told there is
one (Figure~\ref{fig:answers}). The same flag \emph{raises} accuracy on WikiContradict, where both sides are valid.
\item \textbf{Knowing that one side is false is not enough.} A flag that says so, without saying which, lowers
gpt-4o-mini's ConflictBank accuracy by \azSgMiniOracleFlagOnewrongConflict\ points. It helps only a strong model on
RAMDocs, where the other passages reveal which side is false. An instruction to drop unreliable passages only cancels
the neutral flag's harm.
\item \textbf{Naming the false side helps every generator}, by \azSgTerraOracleFlagDirectionalConflict--\azSgLunaOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\
points on ConflictBank and by \azMjMiniOracleFlagDirectionalConflict\ with gpt-4o-mini when the misinformation
outnumbers the truth three to one. Naming the correct side instead does as much harm or more.
\item \textbf{With a real detector, the detector must be more reliable than the generator.} A gpt-4o-mini detector
names the false side \cbDirAccLlm\% of the time on ConflictBank, mostly for facts the model does not know. Its
directional flag helps gpt-4o-mini and gpt-6-luna (\zSgMiniLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\ and
\zSgLunaLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict\ points on conflict questions) and hurts gpt-6.1-terra
(\zSgTerraLlmDirFlagDirectionalConflict), whose unaided accuracy is higher (exploratory). Under
majority misinformation its direction is at chance. Astute RAG \citep{wang2025astute} and TrustRAG
\citep{zhou2025trustrag} lose legitimate same-name answers on RAMDocs, and on ConflictBank they do not beat a prompt
that states there is one answer.
\end{itemize}
For conflict-aware RAG, we recommend passing direction rather than a bare warning, and reporting a detector's direction
accuracy against the generator's own accuracy, with effects on conflict-free questions, under the exact format that
feeds the generator. We release the harness, pre-registration and every
run.\footnote{Code, the pre-registration and all run outputs are in the anonymised
supplementary material and will be released publicly on acceptance.}
