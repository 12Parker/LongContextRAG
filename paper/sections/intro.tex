\section{Introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) \citep{lewis2020rag} hands a language model passages that may disagree. Some
disagreements are genuine; others are planted or stale misinformation \citep{zou2025poisonedrag,wang2025madam}.
Prompting a model about conflicts can help \citep{cattan2025conflicts}, and recent systems pair contradiction
detectors with prompts that flag the conflict to the generator \citep{wang2026conflictrag}. They report detection F1
and end-to-end accuracy, but not how one leads to the other. We ask what a flag must say, and what its detector must
get right, for the flag to help against misinformation.

We fix the passages and vary two things independently: the \emph{detector} (an LLM, an oracle built from benchmark
labels, or a synthetic detector of chosen quality) and the \emph{format} that passes its output to the
generator, from a neutral ``these passages may conflict'' flag to a \emph{directional} flag naming the likely
misinformation. Answer keys that state a wrong answer without marking any passage control for what a flag conveys.
We run seven generators on six benchmarks, chiefly RAMDocs \citep{wang2025madam} and ConflictBank
\citep{su2024conflictbank}, and registered hypotheses in stages, most after earlier results were known
(\S\ref{sec:setup}).

The paper makes three claims, each scored by string matching:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A neutral flag mainly shifts the answer policy} (\S\ref{sec:neutral}). Asked for one answer, four
of seven generators abstain more (gpt-4o-mini on misinformed and correct answers alike); asked for every valid
answer, they add the misinformation. The other three barely move or move the other way, and where misinformation
passages outnumber the correct one the flag can raise selective accuracy. H2 and H8 were registered before any
confirmatory data, H15 and H21 after earlier results; risk--coverage counts are exploratory.
\item \textbf{Generators defer to directional flags, so a wrong flag can cost more than a right one gains}
(\S\ref{sec:direction}). With direction randomised on true contradictions (H31, registered before its runs but
after earlier results), three of four generators follow wrong flags about as often as right ones, and the most
accurate of the four follows nearly all of them; the implied break-even direction precision is exploratory.
\item \textbf{A frozen account of these follow rates predicts within a benchmark and shows where it fails}
(\S\ref{sec:real}). Predictions committed before any flagged answer existed came close on unseen ConflictBank
questions (H32, prospective), though a baseline that ignores which questions are flagged was nearly as close. They
overstated harm on clean questions and on HealthContradict, whose labels are unverified.
\end{itemize}

The numbers behind claims 2 and 3 come from ConflictBank, whose misinformation is
LLM-written about facts the generators rarely know, and whose false side a bag-of-words classifier picks from
passage style alone (\S\ref{sec:setup}). Detectors and generators may therefore find the false side from style, not
from knowledge or reasoning about the contradiction. HealthContradict and RAGuard use real documents but serve only
as label-transfer stress tests, because their labels, not verified facts, decide which side counts as false.
Whether a detector's verdict passed as an answer key conveys as much as its flag depends on the scorer and is
unresolved (\S\ref{sec:open}). We make no claims about model families. We release the harness, the
pre-registration and every run.\footnote{Code, the pre-registration and all run outputs are in the anonymised
supplementary material and will be released publicly on acceptance.}
