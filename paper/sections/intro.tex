\section{Introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) \citep{lewis2020rag} hands a language model passages that may disagree. Some
disagreements are genuine: two sources report different findings, or two people share a name. Others are planted or
stale misinformation \citep{zou2025poisonedrag,wang2025madam}. A natural fix is to detect the contradiction and tell
the generator about it. Recent systems pair pairwise contradiction detectors with prompts that flag conflicting
passages \citep{wang2026conflictrag}, and prompting a model about conflicts can help \citep{cattan2025conflicts}.
These systems are judged mainly by detection F1 and end-to-end accuracy, which hides two questions. What should the
flag say? And what must the detector get right for the flag to help?

We separate the two. We fix the retrieved passages and vary, independently, the \emph{detector} (a heuristic, an NLI
cross-encoder, an LLM, a random placebo and an oracle) and the \emph{format} that passes its output to the generator:
a neutral ``these passages may conflict'' flag, a \emph{directional} flag that names the likely misinformation, or
removal of the suspect passage. To see what detector quality buys, we also build synthetic detectors with a chosen
precision, recall and direction accuracy. We ran pilots on 200 RAMDocs questions \citep{wang2025madam}, then
pre-registered our hypotheses and tested them on 300 held-out questions with two generators, plus a contrast
benchmark of genuine conflicts \citep{hou2024wikicontradict}.

Our findings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A correct neutral flag can hurt.} Flagging exactly the true contradictions lowers gpt-4o-mini's
accuracy on RAMDocs conflict questions by \adOracleFlagConf\ points \dOracleFlagConfCI. The model reads ``these
conflict'' as ``both are valid answers'' and repeats the misinformation. The same flag \emph{raises} accuracy on
WikiContradict, where both sides are valid, by \awikiOracleFlag\ points (\awikiGptOracleFlag\ with gpt-4.1).
\item \textbf{Direction is what helps.} Saying which passage is likely wrong gains \adOracleDirConf\ points on
conflict questions, and removing that passage gains \adOracleRemConf. A real gpt-4o-mini detector with directional
output gains \adLlmDirConf\ (directional flag) and \adLlmRemConf\ (removal).
\item \textbf{Removal is the high-variance choice.} The detector's false flags remove correct passages and cost
\adLlmRemClean\ points on conflict-free questions, cancelling the gain overall (\dLlmRemAll). A directional flag costs
little on clean questions, so it is the safer interface.
\item \textbf{Pair-level F1 does not predict benefit.} A detector's effect is predicted far better by what it does to
\emph{documents}: whether each question's misinformation is removed and whether a correct document is lost. The
heuristic detector we started from does no better than random flags of the same volume.
\end{itemize}
Together these results suggest a reporting standard: evaluate a conflict detector by its document-level behaviour on
both conflict and conflict-free questions, under the exact format it will feed the generator. We release the harness,
pre-registration and every run.\footnote{Code, the pre-registration and all run outputs are in the anonymised
supplementary material and will be released publicly on acceptance.}
