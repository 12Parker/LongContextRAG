\section{Introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) \citep{lewis2020rag} hands a language model passages that may disagree. Some
disagreements are genuine: two sources report different findings, or two people share a name. Others are planted or
stale misinformation \citep{zou2025poisonedrag,wang2025madam}. A natural fix is to detect the contradiction and tell
the generator. Recent systems pair contradiction detectors with prompts that flag conflicting passages
\citep{wang2026conflictrag}, and prompting a model about conflicts can help \citep{cattan2025conflicts}. These systems
report detection F1 and end-to-end accuracy, but not how the two connect. What should the flag say, and what must the
detector get right for it to help?

We fix the retrieved passages and vary, independently, the \emph{detector} (heuristic, NLI, LLM, random placebo,
oracle, and synthetic detectors of chosen quality) and the \emph{format} that passes its output to the generator: a
neutral ``these passages may conflict'' flag, a flag that says one side is false without saying which, a
\emph{directional} flag that names the likely misinformation, or removal of the suspect passage. After pilots on
RAMDocs \citep{wang2025madam}, we pre-registered our hypotheses (H1--H9) and tested them on 300 held-out questions;
seven addenda (H10--H29), each committed before its runs, extended the study to ConflictBank \citep{su2024conflictbank}
(including a variant where the misinformation outnumbers the truth), to RAGuard \citep{zeng2025raguard}, whose
misleading side is real Reddit posts, and to seven generators from OpenAI, Anthropic and Qwen. Controls separate what a
flag conveys: a forced-choice prompt with no ``unknown'', an answer key that names the wrong answer without pointing at
any passage, the detector model's own answer given as a hint, and a crossed design in which six detectors serve six
generators. WikiContradict \citep{hou2024wikicontradict}, whose conflicts are genuine, is the contrast case.

Our findings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A correct neutral flag trades misinformation for abstention.} It moves misinformed answers, and some
correct ones, into ``unknown'' and barely changes accuracy on the answers that remain. Its effect on strict accuracy
ranges from harmful to slightly helpful across \nNeutralSettings\ settings (prediction interval \neuPI); when a
misinformed answer is costed at four correct ones, it helps in \nUtilNeuPosFour\ settings and hurts in
\nUtilNeuNegFour.
\item \textbf{An oracle's direction is a ceiling.} Naming the false side helps on every benchmark that labels one, but an answer key
that names the wrong answer does nearly as well (H23), so the oracle rows bound what any detector can deliver.
\item \textbf{With a real detector, its errors decide.} With a gpt-4o-mini detector the registered gains depend on the
generator: on ConflictBank \nRealSgPos\ of \nRealSgTests\ generators gain significantly and \nRealSgNeg\ loses. Across \hTwentyFourCells\
crossed detector--generator cells, the registered reliability gap correlates with the gain, but almost entirely
through the generator's own accuracy. What matters is where the detector errs: generators follow a wrong-side flag
more readily than a right one, so a detector helps a generator only if it is right where that generator is wrong and
silent where it is right. The detector model's own answer as a hint does not reproduce the gain (H27), and reversing or
paraphrasing the passages leaves direction unchanged (H29).
\item \textbf{Direction is unsafe on genuine conflicts.} On WikiContradict the LLM directional flag, and removal, cut
the share of answers that give both valid sides (H28).
\end{itemize}
For conflict-aware RAG, we recommend measuring a detector's wrong-side rate on questions the target generator already
answers correctly, letting the detector abstain, passing direction only where conflicts are likely to be
misinformation, and treating a neutral flag as a caution signal whose value depends on the cost of a wrong answer. We
release the harness, pre-registrations and every run.\footnote{Code, the pre-registration and all run outputs are in
the anonymised supplementary material and will be released publicly on acceptance.}
