\section{Introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) \citep{lewis2020rag} hands a language model passages that may disagree. Some
disagreements are genuine: two sources report different findings, or two people share a name. Others are planted or
stale misinformation \citep{zou2025poisonedrag,wang2025madam}. A natural fix is to detect the contradiction and tell
the generator about it. Recent systems pair pairwise contradiction detectors with prompts that flag conflicting
passages \citep{wang2026conflictrag}, and prompting a model about conflicts can help \citep{cattan2025conflicts}.
These systems report detection F1 and end-to-end accuracy, but not how the two connect. What should the flag say,
and what must the detector get right for it to help?

We separate the two. We fix the retrieved passages and vary, independently, the \emph{detector} (a heuristic, an NLI
cross-encoder, an LLM, a random placebo and an oracle) and the \emph{format} that passes its output to the generator:
a neutral ``these passages may conflict'' flag, a \emph{directional} flag that names the likely misinformation, or
removal of the suspect passage. To see what detector quality buys, we also build synthetic detectors with a chosen
precision, recall and direction accuracy. After pilots on 200 RAMDocs questions \citep{wang2025madam}, we
pre-registered our hypotheses and tested them on 300 held-out questions with three generators and two prompt
skeletons. A second registration added ConflictBank \citep{su2024conflictbank}, which had no pilot, two published
conflict-robust methods, and a neutral flag with an explicit resolution instruction. WikiContradict \citep{hou2024wikicontradict}, whose conflicts are genuine, is the contrast
case. Our claims concern injected misinformation scored by strict exact match; we do not claim they carry over to
open-ended answers or to conflicts in which both sides are valid.

Our findings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A correct neutral flag does not help against misinformation.} Flagging exactly the true contradictions
lowers gpt-4o-mini's accuracy on RAMDocs conflict questions by \adOracleFlagConf\ points \dOracleFlagConfCI. The
estimate is negative in every setting we ran: three generators and two prompt skeletons on RAMDocs, and three
generators on ConflictBank, where all three drops are significant. Telling the model to ignore unreliable passages
only cancels the harm. The same flag \emph{raises} accuracy on WikiContradict, where both sides are valid, by
\awikiOracleFlag\ points (\awikiGptOracleFlag\ with gpt-4.1).
\item \textbf{Acting on direction helps.} Removing the passage the oracle names as misinformation gains on conflict
questions in every setting (\axQwenMinOracleRemConf--\agptOracleRemConf\ points on RAMDocs,
\azCbQwenOracleRemoveSuspectConflict--\azCbFourOracleRemoveSuspectConflict\ on ConflictBank). A directional flag
beats the neutral flag everywhere. It beats no flag on ConflictBank for all three generators, and on RAMDocs for the
generators that follow the warning (gpt-4.1; gpt-4o-mini with the default prompt). Naming the wrong side costs \aadvDirConf\ points as a flag and
\aadvRemConf\ with removal.
\item \textbf{Removal is the high-variance choice.} False flags make the model lose correct passages. With a real LLM
detector, removal's \adLlmRemConf-point gain on conflict questions is offset by a \adLlmRemClean-point loss on clean
ones, in every generator.
\item \textbf{With a real detector, direction is competitive with published methods.} A gpt-4o-mini detector's
directional flag, in one generator call, beats Astute RAG \citep{wang2025astute} and TrustRAG
\citep{zhou2025trustrag} on labelled RAMDocs questions by \hFourteenAstMini\ and \hFourteenTrMini\ points; their
consolidation step loses legitimate same-name answers. On ConflictBank it ties them. A Qwen2.5-7B detector
gives the same picture. The heuristic detector we started from does not beat random flags of the same volume, and a
grid of synthetic detectors predicts real detectors' effects only roughly.
\end{itemize}
For conflict-aware RAG, we recommend reporting direction accuracy and effects on conflict-free questions alongside
detection F1, under the exact format that feeds the generator. We release the harness, pre-registration and every
run.\footnote{Code, the pre-registration and all run outputs are in the anonymised
supplementary material and will be released publicly on acceptance.}
