\section{Framework}
\label{sec:method}

A question comes with retrieved passages. A \emph{detector} returns flagged pairs of passages and, if it is
directional, a \emph{suspect} side for each pair: the passage it judges false. A \emph{format} turns
the passages and the detector's output into the generator's prompt. Every prompt ends with an instruction to give a
\texttt{Final answer(s):} line, and states an answer policy. The \emph{multi-answer} skeleton asks for every valid
answer when the passages support several; the \emph{one-answer} skeleton says the question has exactly one; the
\emph{minimal} skeleton states no policy. All three allow ``unknown''; the \emph{forced} skeleton is one-answer
without it (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}).

\paragraph{Formats.}
\emph{Plain} gives passages and question only. The \emph{neutral flag} heads each flagged passage with
\texttt{[POTENTIAL CONFLICT with passage(s): j]} and lists the conflicting pairs. The \emph{directional flag}
instead heads suspect passages with \texttt{[LIKELY MISINFORMATION; conflicts with passage(s): j]}. \emph{Remove}
deletes the suspect passages. Controls separate what a flag conveys: a one-wrong flag (one side is false, not
which), an \emph{answer key} and a \emph{verdict key} (Table~\ref{tab:terms}); pick and hint
controls are in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}. A \emph{single call} names suspect passages and
answers in one prompt; \emph{closed book} gives no passages.

\paragraph{Detectors.}
The \emph{LLM detector} is one call that lists contradicting pairs as JSON, separating them from ambiguity,
complementary and temporal relations, and names the likelier misinformation side (or ``both'' or ``unclear''). It
runs on gpt-4o-mini (temperature 0) unless stated, and on other models in the crossed and prospective designs. The \emph{oracle}
flags exactly the gold contradiction pairs, as the benchmark's labels define them, and names the labelled
misinformation side. A \emph{synthetic detector} is built from gold labels with a chosen pair precision, recall and
direction precision $d$: each flag on a true pair names the false side with probability $d$, independently per
question. The randomised test (H31) uses $d=0.5$ on every true pair. The heuristic CAAG detector, NLI, a random
placebo and a 36-cell synthetic grid are in Appendices~\ref{app:extra} and \ref{app:grid}.

\paragraph{The follow-rate account.}
A right flag is \emph{followed} when the flagged answer is correct where the no-flag answer was wrong; a wrong flag
is followed when the flagged answer contains the misinformation answer (matched in either direction) where the
no-flag answer was correct. Abstaining is neither (\prs{16.3}). Generator $G$'s follow rates
$f_R^G$ and $f_W^G$ are these shares in the randomised test. For detector $D$ and question $i$, let $r_i=1$ if $D$
flags the true pair and names its false side, $w_i=1$ if $D$ marks a correct passage as suspect, and $a_i=1$ if
$G$'s no-flag answer is correct. Over $n$ questions, the account predicts the directional flag's effect as
\begin{equation}
\hat\Delta(G,D)=\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_{i}\big[r_i(1-a_i)f_R^G-w_ia_if_W^G\big].
\label{eq:account}
\end{equation}
Unflagged questions keep their no-flag answer, so they contribute zero. The equation applies $f_W$, measured as
adoption, as the probability of losing a correct answer. Every term except the two rates is observed
before any flagged answer exists. Equation~\eqref{eq:account} is therefore bookkeeping plus one empirical claim: that
a generator's follow rates transfer across detectors and questions. It omits right flags on correct answers, wrong
flags on wrong answers, and abstention.
