\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Knowledge conflicts.}
Language models can prefer memorised knowledge over contradicting context \citep{longpre2021entity}, yet are readily
persuaded by coherent contradicting evidence \citep{xie2024chameleon}, to a degree that depends on the strength of
their prior \citep{wu2024clasheval}; \citet{huang2025trust} train models to decide which of context and memory to
trust, including on real incorrect Reddit contexts, and context-faithful prompting changes how readily models follow
or abstain on conflicting context \citep{zhou2023context}. Of the conflict types of \citet{xu2024survey}, we study inter-context
conflicts, as in \citet{chen2022rich}, who find that models tend to rely on a single passage when evidence conflicts,
and \citet{wan2024convincing}, who find that models weigh a passage's relevance more than its credibility cues.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
RAMDocs \citep{wang2025madam}, built on AmbigDocs \citep{lee2024ambigdocs}, mixes genuine ambiguity, misinformation
and noise. CONFLICTS \citep{cattan2025conflicts} annotates search results with conflict types, each with its own
desired behaviour, and WikiContradict \citep{hou2024wikicontradict} pairs genuinely contradicting Wikipedia passages.
FaithEval \citep{ming2025faitheval}, ConflictBank \citep{su2024conflictbank}, ConfRAG \citep{yuan2026confrag},
QACC \citep{liu2025qacc} and WhoQA \citep{pham2024whoqa} cover related settings. RAMDocs and ConflictBank label which side is misinformation; WikiContradict does not.

\paragraph{Telling the model about conflicts.}
Prompting a model to reason about the conflict type helps, and supplying the gold type helps much more
\citep{cattan2025conflicts}; on WikiContradict, models acknowledge both sides more often when told the passages may
conflict \citep{hou2024wikicontradict}. Ragability \citep{gross2026ragability} finds that such a hint helps on
contradictory contexts but hurts on \emph{consistent} ones, where it is false. In our neutral-flag result the hint is
true and the input contradictory, and accuracy still falls, because the hint does not say which side to trust.
\citet{pan2023risk} test detection and ``vigilant'' prompting against LLM-generated misinformation, and
\citet{pan2023attacking} call for misinformation-aware QA. Source metadata and credibility signals also shape how models resolve conflicts
\citep{chiang2024metadata,ge2025confact}.

\paragraph{Telling the model which passage is wrong.}
Closest to our directional format, \citet{hong2024gullible} pass a discriminator's marks on perturbed passages to the
generator in the prompt, which makes it more robust to counterfactual noise. CrAM \citep{deng2025cram} rescales
attention by passage credibility, RA-RAG \citep{hwang2025rarag} estimates source reliability, and
\citet{weller2024defending} defend open-domain QA against disinformation. TACS \citep{yu2024tacs} detects untruthful context and masks it,
close to our removal format. FaithfulRAG \citep{zhang2025faithfulrag} models
conflicts at the level of facts before generating. These works, with \citet{pan2023risk}'s finding that vigilant
prompts give limited protection, already show that a reliability signal can help more than a warning, and some vary
the signal's quality \citep{hong2024gullible,pan2024credibility}. What we add is a randomised manipulation of
direction on the same pairs, answer-key controls that separate the verdict from the marking, its cost on
conflict-free questions, and a break-even account of when a real detector's verdict helps a given generator. RGB \citep{chen2024rgb} warns models that passages may contain errors, and they still rarely
detect them; our neutral flag is the pair-specific version of that warning. Models also follow injected hints and asserted views, wrong
ones included \citep{turpin2023unfaithful,sharma2024sycophancy}, and advice improves a decision-maker only when its
errors fall where the decision-maker is right less often \citep{bansal2021accurate,mozannar2020defer}, which is the
condition our break-even makes quantitative. Whether
abstaining is a failure depends on the cost of a wrong answer \citep{wen2025abstention,song2025trustscore}.

\paragraph{Detection, resolution and poisoning.}
ConflictRAG \citep{wang2026conflictrag} trains a pairwise detector with an LLM fallback and reports pair-level F1
alongside answer gains; its NLI and chain-of-thought baselines resemble two of our detectors. LLMs detect conflicts but struggle to pinpoint them
\citep{gokul2025contradiction,wang2024resolving,jiayang2024econ}, favour the more frequent side, the one matching
their prior and LLM-generated passages \citep{jin2024tugofwar,kortukov2024realistic,tan2024blinded}, and are
distracted by irrelevant context \citep{shi2023distracted,schuster2026whose}. TCR \citep{ye2026tcr}, context-aware
decoding \citep{shi2024cad}, EvidentialRAG \citep{hossain2026evidentialrag} and credibility-aware generation
\citep{pan2024credibility} learn or decode around unreliable passages, and MADAM-RAG \citep{wang2025madam}, Astute RAG
\citep{wang2025astute}, Self-RAG \citep{asai2024selfrag}, Corrective RAG \citep{yan2024crag} and InstructRAG
\citep{wei2025instructrag} handle them without an explicit contradiction detector. A few injected passages can steer a RAG
system \citep{zou2025poisonedrag}; RobustRAG \citep{xiang2024robustrag} isolates passages for certifiable robustness,
and TrustRAG \citep{zhou2025trustrag} filters suspected poisoned ones, the analogue of our removal. We ask instead how
a detector's output should be passed to the generator.
