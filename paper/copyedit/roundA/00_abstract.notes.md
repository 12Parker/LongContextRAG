## Issues for the authors (summaries checked against results.tex)
1. **Pooled neutral-flag effect drops "exploratory" and the post-hoc coding.** Abstract: "Its effect depends on the answer policy and the model family; pooled over \neuMlK\ settings, allowing for shared questions, it is \neuMlMu\ points \neuMlCI." Source (results.tex, "Across settings"): the multilevel model is labelled "exploratory", and the policy/family splits are "codings chosen after seeing data". The abstract presents both without either qualifier.
2. **Oracle-vs-answer-key equivalence drops "exploratory" and the per-comparison caveat.** Abstract: "Naming the false side helps, but on average no more than an answer key naming the wrong answer (pooled difference \PoolTwentyThree\ points, equivalent within $\pm$3 on average)." Source (results.tex, sec:direction): the pooled bootstrap is "exploratory", and "equivalence holds in only \hTwentyThreeEqN\ of the \hTwentyThreeK\ comparisons alone". The intro at least says "which vary individually"; the abstract has no such caveat. Also, "Naming the false side helps" does not say this is the *oracle* (the source calls these rows "a ceiling, not a detector result"); it is only implied by the preceding "perfectly accurate" sentence.
3. **Break-even values are exploratory in the source but not in the abstract.** Abstract: "help only above a break-even direction precision, which rises with the generator's accuracy from \beMin\ to \beMax." Source: "(Table~\ref{tab:breakeven}; exploratory)", and the rise is "mainly because an accurate generator has more to lose", with follow rates overlapping "except gpt-6.1-terra's".
4. **"Follow rates ... account for the flag's effect" may credit the follow rates more than the source does.** Abstract: "the follow rates from this test account retrospectively for the flag's effect in \predEightN\ crossed detector--generator cells ($r=\predEightR$, mean error \predEightMae\ points; exploratory)." Source: the account "tracks" the cells "mostly through the cell-level rates (\predMargMae\ with cell means alone)" [1.6 vs 1.5] and "still misses the Haiku cell". The abstract does say "combined with each cell's coverage and no-flag accuracy", but a reader will take the follow rates as doing the work; the source says they add little beyond the cell-level rates.
5. **"Matches the flag within 3 points" is stronger than the per-cell source.** Abstract: "passed as an answer key, it matches the flag within 3 points." Source (H30): per-cell differences run \hThirtyAMin\ to \hThirtyAMax\ (the maximum, $+$3.3, exceeds 3); the $\pm$3 equivalence holds for the *pooled* estimate over \PoolThirtyAK\ cells, and appendix Table claims (line 51) marks that pooled TOST "unregistered". The abstract has neither "pooled"/"on average" nor "exploratory".
6. **Rare-conflict claim is an unregistered re-analysis.** Abstract: "when conflicts are rare a real detector's false flags on clean questions can outweigh its gains." Hedged with "can", which fits the source (negative in \prevNegFive\ of \prevN\ settings, significantly in \prevSigNeg), but the claims table (appendix line 52) lists it as "unregistered re-analysis" and the abstract opens by calling the hypotheses "pre-registered". Consider whether a reader will assume this one is registered.
7. **Quoted neutral-flag wording differs across sections.** Abstract: ``these passages conflict''; intro: ``these passages may conflict''; method's actual rendered text: `possible contradiction`. These are inside quotation marks, so I left them; the authors should pick one.
