## Issues for the authors
1. "follow rate" is never defined in the main text before it is used: "Right- and wrong-flag follow rates overlap (descriptive; Table~\ref{tab:breakeven}) except gpt-6.1-terra's" (and in the Figure 1 caption). It needs a short definition (e.g. the share of flagged questions on which the answer follows the flag). I could not add it without adding words.
2. Utility sentence, unclear: "If abstention is costless and a misinformed answer costs $c$ wrong ones, the flag lowers utility significantly in \nUtilSixNegZero\ of \nUtilSixSettings\ settings at $c=0$, and raises it in \nUtilSixPosFour\ at $c=4$". Does "costs $c$ wrong ones" mean "counts as $c$ wrong answers"? And does "raises it" mean significantly?
3. H20, ambiguous: "the flag has no significant effect for gpt-4o-mini (...), gpt-6-luna (...) or gpt-6.1-terra (...), equivalence not established, and a small one for Claude Haiku (\hTwentyFcHaikuFlag)." Is "a small one" a significant effect? The dangling "equivalence not established" also reads awkwardly.
4. Significance not stated: "It helps all \nRealMjTests\ generators under majority misinformation (...), and on RAGuard the two Claude models (H26: ...) but no OpenAI one."; "That hint does worse than the flag in \nHTwentySevenAPos\ of \nHTwentySevenATests\ cells and never better (H27)." Please say whether these are Holm-significant counts.
5. Same detector, two precisions: "the gpt-4o-mini detector's precision (\selHaikuMiniPrec\%)" (69) and "The least precise detector (gpt-4o-mini, \dirPrecMini\%)" (68.8). If they are the same metric, use one macro. If not, name the difference. Also, "comparing precision with $p^\star$": $p^\star$ is a break-even *direction* precision. Say which precision is meant.
6. Registration labels missing where similar results carry one: the H30 pool "pooled over the \PoolThirtyAK\ cells the flag is ahead by \PoolThirtyA\ \PoolThirtyACI, equivalent within $\pm$3" has no "exploratory" (the H23 pool does); the prevalence paragraph ("At a 5\% share of conflict questions ...") has none, although Table~\ref{tab:claims} lists it as an unregistered re-analysis; the matched-policy TrustRAG comparison ("the LLM directional flag beats TrustRAG by \blHFourteenCbs\ points ...") has none, although Setup says the §17 re-analyses are exploratory.
7. "its flag's estimate is negative in \prevNegFive\ of \prevN\ correlated settings (bootstrap \prevNegFiveCI; significantly in \prevSigNeg, positive in \prevSigPos". "Correlated settings" is unexplained. "positive in \prevSigPos" presumably means "significantly positive" (macro name), but the parallel is broken. Fixing it adds words.
8. The paragraph "Genuine conflicts and baselines." opens with H29 ("Reordering ... or paraphrasing passages changes the detectors' hit rate insignificantly (H29)"), an artefact probe that is neither a genuine conflict nor a baseline. I kept the lead-in as a topic label because the paragraph has three unrelated findings. Consider moving H29 or relabelling.
9. The Figure 1 caption says "the flag's effect predicted from H31's follow rates", but the account also uses each cell's coverage and no-flag accuracy (text, §\ref{sec:real}).
10. "Pooled effects vary by policy." The new lead-in states the policy split, which is coded after seeing the data ("codings chosen after seeing data" stays in the paragraph). If the lead-in should not state a post hoc split, revert it to a topic label.
11. "changes accuracy insignificantly" (H25/H26, H29): "insignificantly" is a loose form of "not significant"; "does not significantly change" would match the glossary (+1 word each).
12. "its flags on a correct passage (\selHaikuMiniWrongShare\% of questions) change accuracy there by \selHaikuMiniWrongGain": "flags on a correct passage" presumably means "names a correct passage as suspect" (cf. $w_i$). Consider using the same wording in both places.
