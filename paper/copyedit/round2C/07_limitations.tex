\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Models and detectors.} We test seven generators from three families: four OpenAI models (gpt-4o-mini,
gpt-4.1 and two current reasoning models), Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, and a 4-bit Qwen2.5-7B. Gemini
serves only as a judge, and we run no large open model. Qwen2.5-7B, gpt-6.1-terra and Claude Sonnet ran with one seed,
as did gpt-4.1 outside RAMDocs. The reasoning and Claude models ran only at default temperature, sampling nondeterministically. Claude ran through Anthropic's OpenAI-compatible endpoint. The LLM detector is a single
prompt. The published baselines are reimplemented (Astute RAG has no official code; restoring TrustRAG's system message
changed little), NLI sees only a sentence-sized window, and we compare against no trained detector such as
ConflictRAG's \citep{wang2026conflictrag}. The crossed reliability test (H24) covers \hTwentyFourCells\
detector--generator cells on one benchmark with six detectors, so detector-level tests have little power (an exact permutation has \hRedoNPerm\ arrangements). The generator's own accuracy dominated the
registered H24 correlation. Concurrent runs gave a detector slightly different outputs across cells (identical for at least \frozenShareMin\% of questions); restricting each cell to those moves no
cell's gain by more than \frozenMaxShift\ points. The follow-rate account was defined after seeing the crossed cells. Its prospective
test (H32) uses unseen questions but the same benchmark, prompt and generators. The account overstates harm on
clean questions and on HealthContradict. The break-even direction precision is a calibration for randomised flags on true pairs, not a validated
rule for selective detectors.

\paragraph{Data and power.} Confirmatory evidence comes from two benchmarks: RAMDocs (300 questions, 139
with conflicts) and ConflictBank (400 sampled, 300 with conflicts). In both, misinformation is
synthetic, made by editing or generating text around a substituted answer; ConflictBank's passages are LLM-written
on both sides. The current reasoning models distrust ConflictBank's passages when judging them
(Appendix~\ref{app:more}), which suggests generated benchmarks may not measure how such models treat real
documents. Reversing and paraphrasing passages do not significantly change the detectors' hit rate (H29), but
equivalence within $\pm$5 points is shown for only one of four changes, so position, surface style and deeper regularities of generated text remain possible sources of the detectors' direction. The majority variant tests one
case where the false side is more numerous, but its extra wrong-side passages are generated for the same substitution,
which a real attacker would vary, and include ConflictBank's temporal and semantic conflicts, which we count as
misinformation. RAMDocs' document labels are imperfect, and its pair rule does not align entities
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}). We exclude 35 hold-out questions whose status depends on
documents we could not label without human annotation, and check that conclusions survive treating those
documents as misinformation. RAGuard's misleading side is real text, but its labels are behavioural (whether GPT-4
judged the claim correctly with the post). An LLM audit finds only \rgAudContradict\% of sampled supporting--misleading
pairs to contradict (Appendix~\ref{app:semantic}), so RAGuard's oracle is a label transfer, not a factual oracle. With
posts in context, generators abstain on \rgAbstMin--\rgAbstMax\% of conflict claims, so accuracy on this
true/false task falls below chance; on answered claims it is \rgAnsMin--\rgAnsMax\%, against
\rgCbMin--\rgCbMax\% closed-book. Every generator is more accurate closed-book than with RAGuard's posts
(gpt-4o-mini \zcRgMiniConflict\% against \zpRgMiniConflict\%), so the posts make a weak retrieval setting.
HealthContradict's pages are real, but its labels are as released: an LLM judge finds the correct page supporting
the gold answer for \hcLabCorrect\% and the misinformation page the wrong answer for \hcLabMis\%.
WikiContradict has genuine conflicts but no misinformation side. CONFLICTS shows no significant effect for any
condition; scored only on items with a reference answer, it can neither confirm nor refute our findings. Our
comparisons detect effects of about \mdeMed\ points with 80\% power, so smaller effects, including small harms on clean
questions, may be missed.

\paragraph{Metric.} RAMDocs ships no scorer. Our primary metric reconstructs the published definition and scores our
MADAM-RAG port close to the published number on the pilot split; as the published subset is unknown, this is not a reproduction. The metric is string matching: it misses paraphrases, and partial answers interact with ``no wrong
answer''. We report the more lenient bidirectional variant throughout and mark conclusions depending on it. An outside-family LLM judge agrees with the matcher on \semAgree\% of \semN\ paired outputs. The matcher's
misses depend on the condition (more under answer keys and directional flags) but move no contrast by more than
\semDidMax\ points (Appendix~\ref{app:semantic}). Under that judge's re-score of all \jdN\ responses, neither pooled equivalence holds within
$\pm$3 (the flag leads by \jdThirtyJ\ and \jdTwentyThreeJ; H38), so those claims hold under the matcher only.
No human labels check either scorer: the paired, blinded audit sheet we release is unlabelled.

\paragraph{Direction and the grid.} Oracle direction is close to an answer key (H23), so oracle rows are ceilings, not findings about detectors. Direction precision is the share of a detector's flags on true contradictions naming the false side; pair precision, the share of its flagged pairs that are
true contradictions. The gpt-4o-mini LLM detector's direction precision is
\dirAccLlm\% on RAMDocs and \cbDirAccLlm\% on ConflictBank. Its per-question hit rate is lower because it also misses pairs. Its directional flag's gain over
all ConflictBank questions with gpt-4o-mini is not significant (H15), and with stronger generators it
hurts. The grid's gain rises with direction accuracy, but the grid cannot separate adjacent levels.
Synthetic false flags are spread uniformly with a random suspect side; real detectors concentrate their errors. The grid predicts real detectors only roughly, and three detectors cannot show whether document- or pair-level
summaries predict better.

\paragraph{Pre-registration.} H1--H9 were registered before the confirmatory run and H10--H38 in nine addenda, after earlier results were known but before their own runs. The only
timestamp is the repository's commit history, not a third-party registry. Holm controls error within each family, not
across the programme: of \fdrN\ registered tests, \fdrHolm\ are significant under Holm and \fdrBH\ under a
Benjamini--Hochberg FDR over all tests (both in the hypothesis tables). Several analyses, labelled as such, were chosen after seeing data: the TOST margin, the single- and multi-answer split, the document-level
placement axes, the post-hoc comparison of detector and generator reliability that H24 later tested, H24's mechanism
score, and the refinement of permutation $p$-values at their floor.

\paragraph{Scope.} We study flags passed to the generator as text. Learned interfaces such as soft prompts or
credibility fine-tuning \citep{ye2026tcr,pan2024credibility}, and retrieval-side filtering, may trade off differently. We test one adversarial case, a detector that always names the wrong side, not an attacker who chooses the flagged pairs.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work uses public benchmarks and commercial model APIs, with no human subjects. RAGuard's passages are
public Reddit posts that can contain usernames; we use them as released, and our released RAGuard audit sheet replaces private users' handles with placeholders. The main
risk is dual use: a false ``likely misinformation'' label, or removing a passage, can
change a RAG system's answers. An inverted oracle that names the correct side as misinformation costs \aadvDirConf\
points on RAMDocs and \azCbMiniOracleInvFlagDirectionalConflict\ on ConflictBank as a flag, and \aadvRemConf\ on
RAMDocs when the passage it names is removed. An adversary who can trigger a deployed detector could exploit this
\citep{zou2025poisonedrag}. We therefore report harm on clean questions alongside every gain, and argue that
systems should measure a detector's document-level behaviour before acting on its flags.
